# WS-40 Grain: acceptance spec

Written before any code change. The problems it targets are in the audit (F1 to F12): a
tiled 128-texel plate, low-frequency mottle instead of grain, a dead Size range, a Roughness
control that changes almost nothing, and colour blotches from the stock grain. Each line
below is a behaviour with a threshold and the command that measures it. **Before** is the
base commit (`397041b`), measured with the same runner.

## How to run

```
export PATH=/opt/swift/usr/bin:$PATH
bash docs/quality/lanes/WS-40/tools/run_spec.sh --out /tmp/ws40-after [--unit-tests] \
     [--sheets docs/quality/lanes/WS-40/evidence --before-renders <before-run>/r]
```

The before run, with its renders for `--before-renders`, is
`/tmp/claude-0/-home-user-lumen/ab185077-8985-5b3f-9cbf-02e2e84b3f1c/scratchpad/ws40/spec/before_run/r`.
If that path is gone, recreate it by running the script at the base commit.

`run_spec.sh` builds the release `lumen-render` (`$(swift build -c release
--show-bin-path)/lumen-render`). It then renders every case with `--no-dither` and measures
it with `tools/grain_metrics.py` (`check`, `envelope`, `scale`). It prints
`PASS`/`FAIL`/`UNIT`/`CI`, the measured number and the threshold for each row, and exits
non-zero on any FAIL. It takes about 4.5 min on 4 cores (the before run took 4 min 22 s). The exact renders (input, long edge,
`--set` keys) are the `jobs()` table in `tools/run_spec.py`. A typical one:

```
B=$(swift build -c release --show-bin-path)/lumen-render
$B render --input <in.lfi> --as-shot-kelvin K --as-shot-tint T [--iso ISO] --no-dither \
   --long-edge LE --set look.grain.amount=A --set look.grain.size=S \
   --set look.grain.roughness=R --out g.png         # base: same without the --set keys
python3 docs/quality/lanes/WS-40/tools/grain_metrics.py check g.png base.png [--crop x,y,w,h]
```

Stocks use `--set look.filmLab.stock=lumen/portra400|lumen/trix400
--set look.filmLab.amount=100 --set look.filmLab.grain.amount=100`. The base is the same
stock with no grain keys. Tri-X 400 is the monochrome stock.

**Definitions.**
- **grain** `d` = Y(grained) − Y(base), Rec.709 luma of the 8-bit sRGB codes.
- **std** is the std of `d` in codes.
- **cl** is the 1/e correlation length of `d`, the mean of x and y, in px.
- **6000 eq** is a flat 0.18 grey frame, 6000 × 1024, generated by the runner. Grain size is
  relative to the long edge, so this strip carries exactly the grain of a 6000 px photo
  (about 24 MP, Lightroom's Size-25 reference scale). It is measured on its 1024² crop at
  x = 2488.
- **mid grey** is code ≈ 118.
- Photos are rendered at their 2400 px decode. The `asShotKelvin/asShotTint/captureISO`
  values come from `corpus/lfi/<id>_*.json`.

### Notes per line

1. **No period.** The acf is the linear, overlap-normalised 2D autocorrelation (`acf_beyond`
   in `grain_metrics.py`). The checked cases are creative Size 0/50/100 × Roughness 0/50/100
   at 6000 eq, Portra 400 and Tri-X 400 at 6000 eq, and creative Size 0/50/100 on the 1024
   flat grey.
   - (a) For every radius L from ⌈3·cl⌉ to 512, the mean acf over the ring of radius L is
     below 0.05 in magnitude. The ring mean is used because a single 2D lag has an
     estimator noise of about 0.01 to 0.03.
   - (b) acf along x and along y at 64, 128, 256 and 512 px is below 0.05 in magnitude, as
     the director requires.
   - (c) No single lag between 3·cl and 512 px exceeds 0.15. A tiled plate scores about 1 at
     its period. Independent grain stays at noise level: the lead's prototype measures
     0.008 to 0.046 on 1024².
2. **No spikes.**
   - (a) spike2d_interp is each 2D periodogram sample divided by the radially averaged
     power, interpolated at that sample's radius. The threshold is < 60. White noise scores
     about 12, coarse structureless grain about 16 to 19 (prototype, 1024²), and the old
     plate about 10⁷. The audit's bin-median version is kept in
     the JSON as `spike2d`, but it reads 50 to 80 on structureless coloured noise because
     the bin median understates the power at a steep bin's inner edge. That is why the
     interpolated version is the spec number.
   - (b) radial_spike is the largest ratio of a radial bin to the median of its ±2
     neighbours. Bins below 10⁻³ of the peak (kernel side lobes) are ignored. The threshold
     is < 2.0.
   - The lead's prototype (proto4, 1024², float, grain radius 0.6 px) does NOT reliably
     pass. spike2d_interp is 45.7 with seed (7,11) and **60.8 with seed (100,200)**. Both
     peaks sit at the same diagonal frequency (±0.279, ±0.279) cyc/px, period 2.53 px,
     which makes it a real lattice artifact of the hash or cell layout, not noise. The
     threshold stays; the builder has to remove the artifact, not tune around it.
3. **Amount.** The std at 6000 eq, Size 25, Amount 0/25/50/75/100 rises strictly. The
   linear fit has R² > 0.99. Amount 0 is an exact identity:
   - At 8 bits, `grain{amount 0, size 80, roughness 10}` against no grain has max code diff
     = 0, on the strip and on the 1024 flat grey.
   - Portra `grain.amount=0` against the stock without grain has max code diff = 0.
   - At float level, unit test `GrainSpecTests.testAmountZeroIsExactIdentity` checks that
     `ReferenceRenderer.render` output with creative grain {0, 80, 10}, and with a stock at
     grain 0, is bit-equal to the output with no grain.
4. **Amplitude and visibility.**
   - (a) std at Amount 100, Size 25, mid grey, 6000 eq must be **10 to 14 codes**. The audit
     guessed 8 to 12. I take the lead's band, for three reasons:
     - Lightroom's 100 is a heavy effect. Practitioners use 15 to 45.
     - With a linear Amount, 10 to 14 at 100 puts Amount 30 at 3 to 4 codes. That is
       visible but tasteful fine grain at 100%.
     - At about 12 codes, Amount 50 (about 6 codes, about 5% of level) matches a fast
       35 mm stock: σ_D ≈ 0.05 gives ≈ 5.7 codes at mid grey.

     Today's 5.9 codes lives in 6 to 36 px blobs. It is not comparable.
   - (b, c) On 6929 at the 2400 px decode (100% of what Lumen shows), Amount 50 must be
     visible as grain:
     - Size 25: std ≥ 2.5 codes and cl ≤ 1.5 px.
     - Size 50: std ≥ 3.0 codes and cl ≤ 1.5 px.

     Lightroom-like behaviour: the grain is defined at full resolution and the 2400 decode
     is its downsample. So its per-pixel std is lower than at 6000 eq (prototype: ×0.55 to
     0.7). These floors still sit above today's 2.2 codes, and the cl ceiling rules out
     blotches (today 14 px).
5. **Luminance envelope.** grey_ramp at 2800, Amount 100, Size 25, 16 bins of base code.
   - The prediction is `grain_metrics.py envelope`: per-pixel predicted std ∝
     √(p(1−p)) · Y · ln10 · dsRGB/dY. Here Y is linear luma, D = −log10 Y, p = D/Dmax and
     Dmax = 4 (creative), averaged as rms over each bin.
   - It is scaled to the measurement by least squares over the bins with n ≥ 2000 and a
     prediction ≥ 25% of the largest.
   - (a) Every used bin is within ±20% of the prediction.
   - (b) The peak bin lies in codes 64 to 191. Bin 0–16 is ≤ 25% of the peak, and bin
     239–255 is ≤ 50% of the peak. That means strongest in the midtones, falling toward
     black and white.
6. **Size.** cl at 6000 eq, Amount 100, Roughness 50, Size 0/25/50/75/100.
   - (a) cl rises strictly with every adjacent ratio > 1.15, so there is no dead range.
     Today Size 0 and 25 are byte-identical at 1024.
   - (b) Size 0 / 25 / 50 / 100 fall in 0.6–0.9 / 1.0–1.5 / 1.6–2.5 / 4.0–5.5 px. These are
     the audit's targets: 0.7 / 1.2 / 2 / 4–5. The prototype with grain radius 0.6 → 6 px
     geometric gives 0.77 / 1.11 / 1.75 / 4.8.
   - The 6000 eq strip is how a 6000 px render is measured cheaply. One strip render takes
     about 6 s.
   - The old code passes 6a at 6000 eq, because its 0.5 px texel clamp only bites below
     about 3000 px. Its dead range shows at 1024 (F5). The spec does not require
     ratio > 1.15 at 1024: resolution-independent grain at Size 0 and 25 is sub-pixel
     there and is correctly band-limited. Line 8 is what makes the preview honest.
7. **Roughness.** 6000 eq, Amount 100, Size 50, Roughness 0/25/50/75/100.
   - (a) std stays within ±15% of Roughness 50.
   - (b) Kurtosis of `d` is non-decreasing (tolerance 0.02) and rises ≥ 0.25 from 0 to 100.
     A sparser, more irregular, clumped field has heavier tails. Prototype: 3.02 → 3.51.
   - (c) The local-energy CV (std/mean of the 8×8-block mean of d²) is non-decreasing
     (tolerance 2%), and the r100/r0 ratio is ≥ 1.2. Clumping makes local strength uneven.
     Prototype: 0.318 → 0.436, ×1.37.
   - The old code passes 7b only because its mottle is sub-Gaussian (kurtosis 2.2 to 2.6).
     7c and V2 are the lines that separate real clumping from a fine-octave weight.
   - The visual line V2 covers what it looks like.
8. **Preview/export.** Flat grey at long edge 1024 against 2048 box-downsampled ×2 and
   4096 box-downsampled ×4, at Amount 100, Size 0/50/100 (`grain_metrics.py scale`).
   - The std ratio is 0.9 to 1.1.
   - Over 0 to 0.25 cyc/px (0 to ½ Nyquist), the power ratio per bin is 0.85 to 1.15 and
     the band-integrated power ratio is 0.9 to 1.1.
   - Radial bins are merged from the low end until each holds ≥ 3000 FFT samples. Without
     merging, the lowest bins have about 300 samples and a ±15% test fails on chance alone.
   - Prototype (float, 512 base, so the estimator noise is about 2× the spec's):
     - ×2: std ratio 0.96 to 1.00, band 0.95 to 0.98, per-bin 0.78 to 1.23.
     - ×4 at Size 0: std 0.97, band 0.90.
   - The ×4 band and the high bins at mid sizes are tight. The pixel aperture has to
     behave like the box average for this to pass.
9. **Isotropy.** cl_x / cl_y is between 0.9 and 1.1 for every case of line 1, plus Size 25
   and 75. Today it is 1.16 (horizontal bars).
10. **Chroma.**
    - (a) Creative grain on grey: std(dR−dG) and std(dB−dG) are < 0.10 × luma std, on the
      strip and on the 1024 flat grey.
    - (b) Creative grain on colour, measured on the colorchecker at 1368, Amount 100,
      Size 100, over pixels with every channel ≥ 64. Luminance-only grain scales R, G and B
      by the same factor. So std(ln R′/R − ln G′/G) and the B/G equivalent are
      < 0.10 × std(ln Y′/Y). The 8-bit rounding floor is about 0.04. Today it is 0.37,
      because a per-channel envelope with one noise value makes chroma speckle.
    - (c) Portra 400 at grain 100, at 6000 eq and at 1024: chroma RG and BG are
      0.10 to 0.50 × luma std. That keeps per-layer colour structure without blotches. The
      audit's 1024 numbers were 0.78 / 0.56.
    - (d) Tri-X (monochrome) has chroma < 0.10 × luma.
11. **Determinism.**
    - (a) Two renders of the same recipe are byte-identical (creative and Portra, 6000 eq).
    - (b) Unit test `GrainSpecTests.testSeedChangesPattern`: with two seeds, the grain
      fields of a 256² frame differ and their correlation is |r| < 0.05. `lumen-render` has
      no seed flag, and adding one is not needed for this.
12. **CPU/GPU parity**, by construction and by CI.
    - The hash constants, the field parameters and the normalisation are declared once in
      LumenCore. The CIKL kernel source gets them by string interpolation from those
      declarations, as `plateEncodeScale` did, so it has no literal copies. The verifier
      checks this by reading the diff.
    - macOS CI `KernelGoldenTests` grain-vs-reference parity passes with max abs diff
      < 1e-3. That is checked on CI, not on Linux.
13. **No plate code.** These must give 0 lines:
    - `grep -rnE "GrainPlateMemo|plateEncodeScale|latticeValue|plateSize|FilmGrainProfile\.sample\(|FilmGrainProfile\.plate\(|\.plate\(channel|renderPixelsPerCell|defaultPersistence" Sources Tests`
    - `grep -n affineTile Sources/LumenPipeline/RenderGraph.swift`
    - `grep -niE "octave|persistence" Sources/LumenCore/Engine/FilmLab.swift`

    The dither plate in `PipelineRenderer` (WS-01) keeps its `affineTile`. The names
    `grainPlate` and `plateLongEdge` may stay, because they are the PipelineRenderer
    interface.

Required unit tests (in `Tests/LumenCoreTests/GrainSpecTests.swift`, run by
`run_spec.sh --unit-tests` as `swift test --filter LumenCoreTests.GrainSpecTests`):
`testAmountZeroIsExactIdentity` (3, float level), `testDeterministic`,
`testSeedChangesPattern` (11b). The builder should also pin lines 1, 3, 6 and 10a at field
level where that is cheap.

## Visual claims (a human judges these)

The sheets come from `run_spec.sh --sheets docs/quality/lanes/WS-40/evidence
--before-renders <before-run>/r`. Each item shows the 100% crop and the grain ×4 (128 + 4d),
before | after.

- **V1. At 100% it reads as film grain, not a texture overlay.**
  - Sheets: `01_flatgrey_6000eq_a100_s25`, `02_p6929_wall_a50_s25`,
    `03_p2545_grass_a50_s25`, `04_p983_trees_snow_a50_s25`, `05_p2348_jacket_a50_s25`.
  - In the after tiles and their ×4 grain there are no repeating features, no soft blobs
    larger than the grain, and no grid, diagonal pattern or horizontal streaks.
  - On the 6929 wall the grain is visible at 100% without the ×4 aid, even across the
    wall, and without colour speckle.
  - On 2348 it is weaker in the deepest black than in the midtones but not cut off.
- **V2. The sweeps are monotonic and mean something.**
  - `06_size_sweep_6000eq` (2× zoom) goes from near-pixel speckle at Size 0 to distinct
    coarse grains at Size 100. Each step is visibly coarser and no two look alike.
  - `07_roughness_sweep_6000eq` (2× zoom) goes from even, fine, uniform grain at 0 to
    irregular, clumpy grain with mixed speck sizes at 100. Overall strength looks the same.
- **V3. Stocks.** In `08_stocks_6000eq`, Portra 400 shows faint colour variation at the
  grain's own scale, with no magenta/green blotches. Tri-X is neutral, and each looks like
  its own stock.

The 2400 px photo crops are the 100% view of what Lumen displays for these decodes. They are
not the 7008 px export. The 6000 eq sheets show the export scale.
