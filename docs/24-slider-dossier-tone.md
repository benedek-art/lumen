## 0. Shared machinery (context for every entry)

**The one engine.** All tonal controls except Exposure compile to a per-pixel gain in stops over the zonal coordinate `t = log2(luminance/0.18)` (`ToneEngine.swift:2–4`), baked to a 1024-sample gain LUT against the LumenLog encoded domain (`ToneEngine.swift:410–445`) with a forward monotone clamp as backstop (`ToneEngine.swift:438–440`). The luminance driving `t` is not pixel luma but an **edge-aware, exposure-independent guided-filter mask**: log-encode luminance, self-guided filter at radius `max(2% of long edge, 2)`, one iteration (`ReferenceRenderer.swift:173–191`; GPU twin at `RenderGraph.swift:489–513`, same ε via `ReferenceRenderer.toneMaskEpsilon` at `RenderGraph.swift:498–500`). The filter is He/Sun/Tang's guided filter in the box-filter formulation, `a = cov/(var+ε)` (`SpatialOps.swift:246–284`), specialized to darktable's self-guided "eigf" where `a = var/(var+ε)`, `b = (1−a)·mean`, which makes it exactly exposure-equivariant (`SpatialOps.swift:286–340`).

- **ε = 0.375 EV contrast threshold (measured knee).** `toneMaskContrastThresholdEV = 0.375` converted to the encoded plane as `(0.375/24)² ≈ 2.44e−4` (`ReferenceRenderer.swift:165–171`). Measured (`ToneMaskEdgeTests.swift:1–14`): the previous bare `ε=0.004` was a 1.52 EV threshold that let Shadows +100 halo a 4 EV edge by 0.497 EV; the naive correction (0.06 EV) made the mask follow ±0.2 EV high-ISO grain at σ 0.090 EV. At 0.375 EV: halo 0.052 EV, noise σ 0.010 EV, ~2× margin each way.
- **Zonal monotonicity solver.** One closed-form scale over the whole Highlights+Shadows+Whites+Blacks sum — smallest ratio `Δcontrast/−Δzonal` over falling intervals, swept at 0.05 EV from 2 stops below the black anchor to 2 above the white — then eased onto the limit with a C¹ soft knee at 0.8 (`ToneEngine.swift:146–248`; `ColorMath.swift:203–218`). Verified: the bake clamp fires on 0/1024 samples for all 242 combinations of five sliders at ±100 (`ToneEngine.swift:420–424`).
- **Downstream picture formation.** The gain field feeds a fixed AgX-class two-power sigmoid display transform whose white/black anchors are owned by Whites/Blacks (`DisplayTransform.swift:11–18, 149–214, 266–276`); the transform saturates at the anchors, so no tone gain can render past display white (`ToneEngine.swift:20–26`, asserted end-to-end by `EngineTests.swift:425`).

**Reference points used throughout:**
- darktable tone equalizer (the engine Lumen's mask is modeled on): [docs.darktable.org/usermanual/4.8/en/module-reference/processing-modules/tone-equalizer/](https://docs.darktable.org/usermanual/4.8/en/module-reference/processing-modules/tone-equalizer/) (fetched via [dtdocs source](https://github.com/darktable-org/dtdocs/blob/master/content/module-reference/processing-modules/tone-equalizer.md)) — EV-band gains over a guided mask; eigf is the default "preserve details" filter, documented as "makes the degree of blurring independent of the exposure"; default smoothing diameter **5%** of the long edge, recommended 1–10% for eigf; edge feathering and mask compensation are user-tunable.
- He, Sun, Tang, *Guided Image Filtering*, ECCV 2010 ([link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-642-15549-9_1](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-642-15549-9_1)); TPAMI 2013 35(6):1397–1409 ([semanticscholar.org](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1b7fc36dc56a400f7a7f4c75e53550bbb6a0a4d8)).
- Adobe on adaptivity: Adobe Help, *Tone Control Adjustment in Lightroom Classic and Adobe Camera Raw* ([helpx.adobe.com/lightroom-classic/desktop/help/tone-control-adjustment.html](https://helpx.adobe.com/lightroom-classic/desktop/help/tone-control-adjustment.html), fetched): documents histogram target areas — Exposure 30–70% levels, Contrast 30–70%, Highlights 70–90%, Shadows 10–30%, Whites 90–100%, Blacks 0–10% — and states twice that results depend on the "**image adaptive behavior of the Tone controls that causes them to interact with each other**." Lightroom's tone controls are image-adaptive; Lumen's are deliberately image-independent. This is the single largest philosophical divergence and it recurs in every verdict below.

## 1. Exposure

**Expected math.** Industry consensus for a scene-referred pipeline: Exposure is a pure linear gain of `2^EV`.
- darktable *exposure* module: "Increase or decrease the **exposure value (EV)**", operating "linear, RGB, scene-referred", up to ±18 EV — [docs.darktable.org/usermanual/4.8/en/module-reference/processing-modules/exposure/](https://docs.darktable.org/usermanual/4.8/en/module-reference/processing-modules/exposure/) (fetched).
- RawTherapee Rawpedia, *Exposure*: "The values of the Exposure Compensation slider (EC) are ISO values. This means **a value of +1 equals one stop of overexposure (+1 EV)**" — [rawpedia.rawtherapee.com/Exposure](https://rawpedia.rawtherapee.com/Exposure) (fetched).
- Adobe (documented): "Exposure values are in **increments equivalent to aperture values (f-stops)**… An adjustment of +1.00 is **similar to** opening the aperture 1 stop" — *How to adjust image tone and color*, [helpx.adobe.com/lightroom-classic/desktop/process-and-develop-photos/image-tone-color.html](https://helpx.adobe.com/lightroom-classic/desktop/process-and-develop-photos/image-tone-color.html) (fetched). Note the hedge "similar to": Adobe does **not** document a pure gain.
- **Where sources disagree.** Lightroom's PV2012 Exposure is *not* a pure gain: it is midtone-weighted with a built-in highlight rolloff. This is **inferred**, not documented — community measurement shows e.g. clipped white stays at white until roughly −1.0 EV of negative Exposure ("What Does the Exposure Slider Actually Do?", Lightroom Queen forums, [lightroomqueen.com/community/threads/what-does-the-exposure-slider-actually-do.12642/](https://www.lightroomqueen.com/community/threads/what-does-the-exposure-slider-actually-do.12642/) *(snippet)*), and Adobe's own tone-control page's zone table assigns Exposure to "Midtone Brightness (30%–70% Levels)". The scene-referred camp (darktable/RT) treats the rolloff as the display transform's job, not the gain's.

**What Lumen does.** `exposureGain = pow(2, clamp(exposure, −10, 10))` (`ToneEngine.swift:300`), fused into the S6 linear matrix upstream of everything (`RenderPlan.swift:127–129`), excluded from the tone gain LUT (`ToneEngine.swift:281–287, 465` — Exposure alone keeps `isIdentity` true, `EngineTests.swift:281–291`). UI range −5…+5 EV (`Recipe.swift:284–288`). The "safe feel" of LR's rolloff is delegated to the display transform's shoulder, which saturates at the white anchor (`DisplayTransform.swift:250–276`). Because the guided mask is exposure-equivariant, an Exposure drag adds ΔEV to the cached mask instead of rebuilding it (`SpatialOps.swift:297–301`).

**Verdict: CORRECT.** Calibration is exact and asserted: `2^EV` to 1e−9 relative accuracy at the stage where it is defined (`AccuracyProbeTests.swift:19–29`; `EngineTests.swift:276–279`), and display-response monotonicity across the sweep is asserted (`AccuracyProbeTests.swift:210–220`). This matches the darktable/RawTherapee consensus exactly and matches Adobe's *documented* claim ("equivalent to f-stops") better than Lightroom itself does. The divergence from LR's *actual* (inferred) midtone-weighted behavior is deliberate, architecturally coherent (scene-referred gain + display-transform shoulder ≈ same felt contract), and is the design darktable's manual explicitly prescribes ("adjust for the mid-tones first… the sigmoid curve pivots around middle gray", [sigmoid docs](https://docs.darktable.org/usermanual/development/en/module-reference/processing-modules/sigmoid/)).

**Gaps** (ranked):
1. **No side-by-side LR-response validation.** docs/04 promises constants "tuned against LrC 15.5 renders of the golden corpus"; no such fixture exists (grep of `Tests/` finds no LR render comparison). A one-time measured comparison of Lumen `+1 EV` vs LR `+1.00` on mid-grey and on a +3 EV highlight would quantify the felt difference of the missing rolloff.
2. Near-clip behavior differs from LR by construction (Lumen darkens clipped whites immediately with −EV; LR holds them ~1 stop, inferred). If LR-refugee muscle memory matters, an optional highlight-weighted exposure variant is the lever — but it would break the exact `2^EV` contract; document rather than change.

## 2. Contrast

**Expected math.** Consensus: a slope increase around a fixed tonal pivot, midtone-weighted, with compressive rolloff at the extremes.
- Adobe (documented): "Increases or decreases image contrast, **mainly affecting midtones**. When you increase contrast, the middle-to-dark image areas become darker, and the middle-to-light image areas become lighter" — [image-tone-color.html](https://helpx.adobe.com/lightroom-classic/desktop/process-and-develop-photos/image-tone-color.html) (fetched). The pivot's location and the curve's shape are **undocumented**; community measurement places LR's anchor near Lab L≈50 (inferred; so recorded in `docs/04-spec-tone.md` §Contrast+Pivot).
- darktable *sigmoid* (documented): contrast "adjust[s] the aggressiveness of the compression **while leaving middle-gray unchanged**" — [sigmoid docs](https://docs.darktable.org/usermanual/development/en/module-reference/processing-modules/sigmoid/) (fetched).
- darktable *color balance rgb* (documented): "at the fulcrum, the contrast curve leaves the luminance unchanged… The fulcrum has a value of **18.45% by default**, which is consistent with the current scene-referred workflow" — [color-balance-rgb docs](https://docs.darktable.org/usermanual/4.8/en/module-reference/processing-modules/color-balance-rgb/) (fetched).
- RawTherapee (documented, disagreeing): "It applies a contrast curve **centered at the average luminance level**" — i.e. an image-adaptive pivot, per channel — [rawpedia.rawtherapee.com/Exposure](https://rawpedia.rawtherapee.com/Exposure) (fetched).
- **Disagreement summary:** pivot at fixed mid-grey (darktable), at image mean (RawTherapee), at an undocumented adaptive anchor ≈ Lab 50 (Lightroom, inferred). Per-channel RGB application (RT, LR film-like) vs luminance-at-constant-hue (darktable cb-rgb) also differs across the field.

**What Lumen does.** Log-space slope around an explicit pivot: `mapped(t) = pivot + (t−pivot)·effective`, `slope = 1 + 0.6·(c/100)` (range 0.4…1.6), with the slope relaxing back to 1 **at the display anchor on the side being mapped** — `reach = whiteAnchorEV − pivot` above the pivot and `pivot − blackAnchorEV` below it, `effective = mix(slope, 1, smoothstep(0.2, 1, |d|/reach))` (`ToneEngine.swift:591–605`; the hold of 0.2 and its measured cost at `ToneEngine.swift:497–535`). Applied as a scalar gain on RGB via pixel luminance (`ReferenceRenderer.swift:196–200`, from the 1-D table baked at `RenderPlan.swift:199`) — a luminance-ratio contrast, not per-channel. Composes additively (in stops) with the zonal windows and the Zones panel (`ToneEngine.swift:629`). This sits *upstream* of the display transform's own log-log contrast (default 1.5, `DisplayTransformParams.contrast`), so the total mid-grey log-log slope is the product.

> **This paragraph described a different curve until the atlas run measured it.** It said the slope relaxes via `smoothstep(4, 12, |t−pivot|)`, exactly identity ≥12 EV from the pivot, and cited `ToneEngine.swift:363–373`. That was true before A1-01, which replaced the two fixed EV constants with the live anchor — because the old window's 4 and 12 had nothing to do with where a highlight actually clips, and at contrast +100 the white anchor mapped to +7.87 EV with 34 of 256 ramp columns rendering at exactly 255. The verdict below does not change: the fixed-point property it turns on holds more strongly now, since `d·1 == d` at the anchor makes it geometry rather than a wide-enough window.

**Verdict: CORRECT**, with one measured, defensible divergence. Mid-grey (pivot 0) is held *exactly* fixed — `contrastMapped(pivot) = pivot` (`EngineTests.swift:243–248`, plus the given measured fact); the slope at the pivot is exactly what the formula promises: 1.6 / 1.3 / 0.4 at ±100/+50/−100 (`EngineTests.swift:268–274`); the ends of the scale are pinned exactly, so contrast can never clip a highlight it wasn't aimed at (`EngineTests.swift:256–264`). This matches darktable's documented fixed-mid-grey contract and the CDL/colorist slope-around-pivot primitive (see §3), and diverges from RT's image-adaptive mean-pivot and LR's undocumented anchor. Divergence is defensible: a fixed, visible pivot is *more* predictable, and the Contrast Pivot control (below) recovers everything adaptivity buys. The ±100 → ×0.4…×1.6 strength mapping is a design choice with no external referent; whether ±100 "feels like" LR ±100 is **UNPROVEN** (no LR corpus comparison exists).

**Gaps:**
1. **Strength calibration vs LrC unmeasured** — same missing golden-corpus comparison as Exposure; the one number to pin is what LR Contrast +50 does to a ±2 EV grey pair vs Lumen's `1+0.6·0.5 = 1.3` slope compounded with display contrast 1.5.
2. LR's contrast is (inferred) applied per-channel film-like, so +Contrast adds saturation; Lumen's luminance-ratio application does not. Lumen's saturation behavior is *better* science (darktable cb-rgb's argument) but will read as "flatter contrast" to an LR refugee. A documented, optional chroma-coupling (like the curve's `Chroma boost`) is the LR-feel lever.
3. **The pivot can be pushed past the anchor, and then Contrast is exactly dead on that side.** The reach is `whiteAnchorEV − pivot`, the pivot clamps to ±4, and Whites pulls the white anchor down — +100 puts it at 3.5 EV. At Whites +67 and above the reach at pivot +4 is zero or negative (measured: the anchor first reaches +4.00 at exactly Whites +67) and `ToneEngine.swift:601` returns the identity for every tone above the pivot: measured, `max |contrastMapped(t) − t|` over t ∈ [4, 8] is 0.000000 across 4,000 samples at 0.001 EV spacing at `Tone(contrast: 100, contrastPivot: 4, whites: 100)`, while below the pivot it is fully live. The code states the decision and its reason at `ToneEngine.swift:479–483` and it is the right answer — a side already past the end of the scale is not somewhere contrast should push — but neither this dossier nor `docs/04-spec-tone.md` §Contrast mentioned it, so a photographer at those settings has no account of why the slider stopped working on the highlights.

## 3. Contrast Pivot

**Expected math.** The colorist primitive: `out = (in − pivot) × contrast + pivot`, the pivot itself unmoved.
- DaVinci Resolve's Contrast+Pivot: "When adding contrast, anything above the pivot point will be pushed up, while anything below it will be pushed down. **The pivot point itself will remain unchanged**" — *How Contrast Actually Works in DaVinci Resolve*, [colourgrade.agency/learn-the-craft/contrast-in-davinci-resolve/](https://colourgrade.agency/learn-the-craft/contrast-in-davinci-resolve/) *(snippet)*; also MixingLight, *The Multiple Personalities of Resolve's Contrast-Pivot Controls*, [mixinglight.com](https://mixinglight.com/color-grading-tutorials/mastering-the-multiple-personalities-of-resolves-contrast-pivot-controls/) *(snippet — notes the Primaries-palette and HDR-palette pivots are different tools with different behavior)*.
- darktable *color balance rgb* "contrast gray fulcrum", default 18.45% scene-referred — [color-balance-rgb docs](https://docs.darktable.org/usermanual/4.8/en/module-reference/processing-modules/color-balance-rgb/) (fetched).
- Lightroom: **no equivalent exists**; LR's anchor is hidden and immovable (inferred ≈ L 50). This control is a Resolve import, not an LR clone — nothing to match.

**What Lumen does.** `pivot = clamp(contrastPivot, −4, +4)` EV relative to mid-grey; the slope formula of §2 is applied around it in log2 space (`ToneEngine.swift:366–372`; wire range documented `Recipe.swift:285–286`). `contrastMapped(pivot) = pivot` exactly for any pivot (`EngineTests.swift:243–248`: pivot −1 held to 1e−9, tones above pushed up, below pushed down). AutoTone sets the pivot to the face-weighted mean when faces exist (`ToneEngine.swift:576`).

**Verdict: CORRECT.** This is precisely the documented Resolve/CDL primitive, denominated in scene EV (the more principled axis — darktable's fulcrum is the same idea in linear percent). The pivot-holds-still and ends-pinned properties are both asserted. One honest caveat: because the *display transform's* contrast pivots at mid-grey always (`DisplayTransform.swift:12–15`), a non-zero Contrast Pivot means the two contrast mechanisms pivot at different points; the composition is still monotone (solver + clamp) but the combined curve shape at, say, pivot +3 with Render contrast 2.1 has no test coverage.

**Gaps:**
1. Interaction test: pivot at ±4 EV × Contrast ±100 × non-default Render presets — assert no plateau wider than N samples in the baked LUT.
2. Resolve's HDR-palette pivot also *scales per-zone ranges*; Lumen's zones axis is anchored by Whites/Blacks instead. Fine — but document that moving Contrast Pivot does not move the Zones axis, since Resolve users may expect coupling.

## 4. Highlights

**Expected math.**
- Adobe (documented): "Adjusts bright image areas. Drag to the left to darken highlights and **recover 'blown out' highlight details**. Drag to the right to brighten highlights while minimizing clipping" ([image-tone-color.html](https://helpx.adobe.com/lightroom-classic/desktop/process-and-develop-photos/image-tone-color.html), fetched); target area "Highlight Area (**70%–90% Levels**)" with "some interaction between the controls", behavior "**image adaptive**" ([tone-control-adjustment.html](https://helpx.adobe.com/lightroom-classic/desktop/help/tone-control-adjustment.html), fetched). PV2012's Highlights/Shadows are edge-aware tone mapping (Eric Chan's account of the algorithm's development, *Tonal Adjustments in the Age of Lightroom 4*, Luminous Landscape, [luminous-landscape.com/tonal-adjustments-in-the-age-of-lightroom-4/](https://luminous-landscape.com/tonal-adjustments-in-the-age-of-lightroom-4/) *(snippet — fetch blocked)*). The actual weight curve is **proprietary and inferred only**.
- darktable tone equalizer (documented): highlight recovery = negative gains on the top EV bands of a guided-mask EV axis, smoothly interpolated ([tone-equalizer docs](https://docs.darktable.org/usermanual/4.8/en/module-reference/processing-modules/tone-equalizer/), fetched).
- RawTherapee (documented, different mechanism): *Highlight Compression* squeezes tones above a threshold ("100 sets the threshold at one stop below the white point, so all the compressed highlights are squeezed into the top stop", [Rawpedia Exposure](https://rawpedia.rawtherapee.com/Exposure), fetched).
- **The feathering question (central).** Adobe's own zone table keeps Highlights *out* of the 30–70% midtone band — i.e. even LR's documentation draws a boundary near the upper midtones — but the ranges are drag-zone definitions, the true response feathers into midtones and is image-adaptive (documented as "interaction", exact extent inferred). darktable's equalizer has no hard zero anywhere: every band's gain crossfades smoothly across the whole axis. **Nobody else has a hard zero at exactly mid-grey.**

**What Lumen does.** A **shelf**: `weight = smoothstep(0, whiteAnchorEV, t)` — exactly 0 at and below mid-grey, full strength at the white anchor **and above it** (`ToneEngine.swift:311–315`, constant `highlightShelfEnd = 1.0` at :66). ±100 ⇒ ±2.0 EV peak gain (`highlightShadowRangeEV`, :44), scaled by the zonal solver only when windows conflict; the applied value is exposed as `effectiveHighlights` (:106–114). The shelf deliberately saturates rather than tapering: a bump that returned to zero at white could not touch a blown sky (:12–18, :304–310); clip-safety moved to the display transform's saturation at the anchor (:20–26; `EngineTests.swift:425`). Shelf max slope 0.6 < 1, so a lone slider is monotone by shape (:62–66). Hard zone partition asserted: `Highlights(+100).stops(at −3) = 0` exactly (`EngineTests.swift:193–198`), weight 0 at mid-grey (`EngineTests.swift:155–169`).

**Verdict: DIVERGES — defensible design, with one real feel risk.** The mechanism (EV-shelf gain on an eigf guided mask) matches the darktable-documented consensus and improves on it (measured ε, no user calibration ritual). The recovery contract (−100 actually reaches and pulls down a blown sky; nothing renders past display white) matches Adobe's documented wording *better than a taper would*. The divergence is the **hard zero at mid-grey**: LR (inferred) and darktable (documented) both feather highlight moves into the midtones; Lumen's smoothstep is C¹ at t=0 so there is no visible hinge, but the *authority distribution* differs — LR's Highlights −100 visibly darkens upper midtones; Lumen's leaves everything at or below mid-grey byte-identical. Defensible (composability: Highlights and Shadows can never fight over mid-grey; Exposure alone owns overall brightness), but it is a measurable departure from the LR hand-feel the spec (docs/04 §Six-Slider) promises to match "within visual tolerance." Also note the spec text still describes the old taper-to-zero geometry ("never moves the white point by construction", `docs/04-spec-tone.md` §Contract semantics) — the code comment (:12–26) says this moved to the transform; **docs/04 is stale here**.

**Gaps** (ranked):
1. **Feathering below mid-grey.** Consider widening the shelf's foot to ~−1 EV (e.g. `smoothstep(−1·k, whiteAnchorEV, t)`) or an opt-in "LR-feel" profile; measure against an actual LrC −60 Highlights render first. This is the single most likely "feels different from Lightroom" source in the panel.
2. No image adaptivity: LR scales highlight recovery by scene content (documented as behavior, mechanism inferred). Lumen's Auto (`ToneEngine.swift:549–553`) is the deliberate substitute — visible slider values instead of hidden adaptation. Keep, but document the trade in the dossier-of-record.
3. Update docs/04's stale taper claim to the shelf + transform-saturation contract (`ToneEngine.swift:12–26` is correct; the spec isn't).
4. Mask radius 2% of long edge vs darktable's 5% default (1–10% recommended for eigf) — a one-shot A/B on the halo/local-contrast trade at 4–5% could be worth it; the current halo number (0.052 EV) is already good.

## 5. Shadows

**Expected math.**
- Adobe (documented): "Adjusts dark image areas… Drag to the right to brighten shadows and recover shadow details" ([image-tone-color.html](https://helpx.adobe.com/lightroom-classic/desktop/process-and-develop-photos/image-tone-color.html), fetched); target area "Shadow Area (**10%–30% Levels**)", image-adaptive, interacting ([tone-control-adjustment.html](https://helpx.adobe.com/lightroom-classic/desktop/help/tone-control-adjustment.html), fetched). Exact curve **inferred only**; known community complaint that ±100 flattens ("faux-HDR"), recorded in `docs/04-spec-tone.md` §Vs-the-field.
- darktable tone equalizer (documented): shadow lift = positive gains on low EV bands, guided mask, local contrast preserved ([tone-equalizer docs](https://docs.darktable.org/usermanual/4.8/en/module-reference/processing-modules/tone-equalizer/), fetched).
- RawTherapee (documented): shadow tools are display-shaped (*Shadow Compression* "dampens the effect of the Black slider", [Rawpedia Exposure](https://rawpedia.rawtherapee.com/Exposure), fetched) — a different, cruder mechanism; included as the "what everyone shipped before guided masks" baseline.

**What Lumen does.** Mirror shelf in ascending form: `weight = smoothstep(0, −blackAnchorEV·0.5, −t)` — 0 at and above mid-grey, full strength at **half** the black anchor (−4.5 EV by default) and below (`ToneEngine.swift:332–342`; `shadowShelfEnd = 0.5` justified at :68–77: saturating at the anchor itself spent the shelf where the display toe leaves nothing to move — measured 9.1 code values of authority at anchor-saturation vs 32.7 at half-anchor). Same ±2 EV range, same solver, same mask. The asymmetry vs Highlights (`shadowShelfEnd` 0.5 vs `highlightShelfEnd` 1.0) is a display-toe-driven design measurement, not a mirror-symmetry break by accident. Ascending-form subtlety (a naive `smoothstep(0, lo, t)` with negative `lo` degenerates to a step and inverts the window) is documented and guarded (:335–341; `ColorMath.swift:226`).

**Verdict: DIVERGES exactly as Highlights does (hard zero at mid-grey), otherwise CORRECT.** Mechanism matches the documented darktable model; authority placement (−4.5 EV full strength ≈ "10–30% levels" region) is consistent with Adobe's documented target area; the anti-halo number (0.052 EV on a 4 EV edge, `ToneMaskEdgeTests.swift:10–11`) is the direct fix for LR's most-documented Shadows failure (halos at backlit edges — Adobe's 2026 mask "Edge" control cited as their own admission in `docs/04-spec-tone.md` §Vs-the-field; **that admission is the project's claim, not verified externally this cycle**). The mid-grey hard zero and non-adaptivity carry the same caveats as §4.

**Gaps:**
1. Same feathering-above-mid-grey question as Highlights (LR's +Shadows visibly lifts lower midtones).
2. The +100 "faux-HDR flatness" claim (Lumen retains micro-contrast because the lift is scene-referred through an edge-aware mask) is architecture-plausible but **unproven against an actual LR render** — add one golden.
3. `effectiveShadows` exists so the UI can show solver easing (`ToneEngine.swift:113–114`); verify the panel actually surfaces it (out of scope for this dossier's files; flag for UI audit).

## 6. Whites

**Expected math.**
- Adobe (documented): "**Adjusts white clipping.** Drag to the left to reduce clipping in highlights. Drag to the right to increase highlight clipping. (Increased clipping may be desirable for specular highlights…)" ([image-tone-color.html](https://helpx.adobe.com/lightroom-classic/desktop/process-and-develop-photos/image-tone-color.html), fetched); "The Whites control **sets the white clipping point**", visible via Alt-drag; target area 90–100% levels ([tone-control-adjustment.html](https://helpx.adobe.com/lightroom-classic/desktop/help/tone-control-adjustment.html), fetched). How much visible tonal change accompanies the clipping-point move is **undocumented/inferred**.
- darktable *filmic rgb* (documented, the scene-referred formalization): "**white relative exposure**: The number of stops (EV) between the scene middle-gray luminance and the scene luminance to be **remapped to display white**… everything brighter… will be clipped (pure white)" — [filmic-rgb docs](https://docs.darktable.org/usermanual/4.8/en/module-reference/processing-modules/filmic-rgb/) (fetched).
- RawTherapee (documented, adjacent): Highlight Compression + threshold define how scene highlights map into the top of the display range ([Rawpedia Exposure](https://rawpedia.rawtherapee.com/Exposure), fetched).

**What Lumen does.** Two mechanisms, both live:
1. **Anchor move (the "clipping point"):** `whiteAnchorEV = 5.0 − 1.5·(whites/100)` feeds the display transform's white anchor (`ToneEngine.swift:119–123, 477–480`; consumed at `DisplayTransform.swift:161–165`). +100 ⇒ scene +3.5 EV maps to display white (more clipping); −100 ⇒ +6.5 EV (less). This is filmic's white-relative-exposure and Adobe's documented "adjusts white clipping", implemented as geometry.
2. **Tonal shelf:** `weight = smoothstep(0.2·hi, 0.8·hi, t)` × up to ±1.3 EV (`ToneEngine.swift:325–329`; `endShelfStart/End` :82–83, `whiteToneEV` :94) — added because the anchor alone measured near-dead (12.3 code values down-travel; Blacks 0.20) since the transform's shoulder had already compressed the region (:317–324; history in `EngineTests.swift:200–212`). Shelf starts above where Highlights has saturated in slope terms so the two controls own different tones (:79–83). Mid-grey provably untouched (`EngineTests.swift:219–220`); real authority at +3.5 EV asserted (:222–223); brightening through the actual transform asserted (`EngineTests.swift:228–241`).

**Response shape (measured, given):** smooth but strongly end-loaded — the last 5 units of travel move at ≈3× the mid-travel rate. Mechanism (inferred from code): the shelf edges are anchor-relative and the anchor itself moves with the slider, so as `whites→100` the shelf slides down toward fixed scene tones *and* the shoulder steepens — two compounding superlinear terms.

**Verdict: CORRECT on the documented contract, UNPROVEN on feel.** "Whites owns the white point" is exactly Adobe's documented semantics and darktable's documented formalization, and Lumen implements it as construction rather than curve-bending — arguably the strongest entry in the panel. The added ±1.3 EV shelf has **no external referent** (LR's Whites tonal response is proprietary); it is justified by internal measurement (a dead slider is broken), sized against the monotonicity budget (:91–94), but its *shape* — including the 3× end-loading — is unvalidated against LR, where community lore expects Whites to respond roughly proportionally. 32-knot interactive cube parks up to 0.080 EV of error on Whites +100 at scene +0.8 EV; the export path uses the 65-knot cube and must at least halve it (`RenderPlan.swift:314–318`; `AccuracyProbeTests.swift:83–110`) — known, bounded, and export-correct.

**Gaps:**
1. **Linearize perceived travel.** The 3× end-loading is the kind of thing LR users report as "the slider jumps at the end." Candidate fix: denominate the anchor move in the *shelf's* frame (freeze shelf edges at default-anchor EVs) or ease the slider→EV mapping; re-measure the travel table (`AccuracyProbeTests.swift:140–222` already prints it).
2. Alt-drag clipping preview (LR's documented affordance for exactly this slider) — spec'd in docs/04, not in the engine files reviewed; ensure the threshold view reads the *anchor*, which is the true clip point.
3. Interactive-vs-export cube divergence (0.080 EV in sky tones) is the largest shipped numeric error in the tone path; the finer interactive cube's bake cost is already instrumented (`AccuracyProbeTests.swift:115–130`) — decide with those numbers.

## 7. Blacks

**Expected math.**
- Adobe (documented): "**Adjusts black clipping.** Drag to the left to increase black clipping (map more shadows to pure black). Drag to the right to reduce shadow clipping" ([image-tone-color.html](https://helpx.adobe.com/lightroom-classic/desktop/process-and-develop-photos/image-tone-color.html), fetched); "sets the black clipping point" with Alt-drag view; 0–10% levels ([tone-control-adjustment.html](https://helpx.adobe.com/lightroom-classic/desktop/help/tone-control-adjustment.html), fetched).
- darktable *filmic rgb* (documented): "**black relative exposure**… scene luminance to be remapped to display black (maximum density)… controls how far the shadows are recovered" — [filmic-rgb docs](https://docs.darktable.org/usermanual/4.8/en/module-reference/processing-modules/filmic-rgb/) (fetched).
- RawTherapee (documented): "Black — Use this to set the black point… Values greater than 0 will make the image darker, negative values will lighten up the shadow parts"; *Shadow Compression* dampens it ([Rawpedia Exposure](https://rawpedia.rawtherapee.com/Exposure), fetched). darktable's *exposure* black-level note (use a curve's toe, not black offset, to add density — [exposure docs](https://docs.darktable.org/usermanual/4.8/en/module-reference/processing-modules/exposure/)) supports anchor-plus-toe over subtractive offsets.

**What Lumen does.** Mirror of Whites: `blackAnchorEV = −9.0 − 1.5·(blacks/100)` into the transform's black anchor (`ToneEngine.swift:120–124`; note the sign — Blacks +100 *deepens* the anchor to −10.5, reducing clipping/lifting shadows; −100 raises it to −7.5, crushing), plus a shelf `smoothstep(0.15·|lo|, 0.62·|lo|, −t)` × up to ±2.2 EV (`ToneEngine.swift:344–349`; `blackShelfStart/End` :85–89, `blackToneEV` :96–98 — larger EV range than Whites because the toe compresses ~3× harder than the shoulder: 1 stop ≈ 4 code values at −5 EV vs 12 at +4). Shelf placed deeper and wider than Shadows' so the two slope peaks don't coincide (Shadows ≈ −2.2 EV, Blacks ≈ −5.9; :85–89). Mid-grey untouched, authority at −4 EV asserted (`EngineTests.swift:213–226`). The `isIdentity` regression where Whites/Blacks-only recipes collapsed the gain LUT to identity is fixed and documented (`ToneEngine.swift:449–468`).

**Verdict: CORRECT on the documented contract (clipping-point ownership = Adobe's text, filmic's math); UNPROVEN on tonal-response feel** for the same reason as Whites — the ±2.2 EV shelf and its placement are internally measured design, with no LR reference measurement. The Whites/Blacks asymmetry (1.3 vs 2.2 EV) is well-reasoned physics of the shared display curve, but it means Blacks −100 and Whites +100 are not mirror images, which LR users may or may not expect — unknown either way, since LR documents neither.

**Gaps:**
1. Same LR-corpus calibration gap as Whites; Blacks is historically the slider Adobe users drag by Alt-view rather than feel, so priority slightly lower.
2. Alt-drag black-clip view keyed to the true anchor (mirror of Whites gap 2).
3. Consider a test pinning the documented direction convention (Blacks +100 lifts / −100 crushes) end-to-end through the transform — `EngineTests.swift:213–226` covers pieces but not a signed display-value assertion at, e.g., −6 EV.

## 8. The Zones panel (dark / shadow / mid / light / bright EV + pivots)

**Expected math.** The model is documented twice in the field:
- **Resolve's HDR Grade palette** (the explicitly named model, docs/04 §Zones): tonal zones with **exposure denominated in stops**, wheels, saturation, and **user-adjustable zone boundaries and falloff** — "The HDR palette splits the image into six overlapping tonal ranges: dark, shadow, light, highlight, specular, and global… you're given the ability to control the start point, overlap, and fall off of those zones" — Blackmagic Design color page ([blackmagicdesign.com/products/davinciresolve/color](https://www.blackmagicdesign.com/products/davinciresolve/color)) and MixingLight, *How Do Zones Work in Resolve 17's New HDR Palette?* ([mixinglight.com](https://mixinglight.com/color-grading-tutorials/how-zones-work-in-resolve-17-hdr-palette/)) *(snippets)*.
- **darktable tone equalizer** (documented, fetched): nine fixed bands at −8…0 EV of *guided-mask* brightness, gains smoothly interpolated with adjustable curve smoothing; mask exposure/contrast compensation re-centers the histogram under the control points ([tone-equalizer docs](https://docs.darktable.org/usermanual/4.8/en/module-reference/processing-modules/tone-equalizer/)).
- Lightroom: **no equivalent exists** (its Color Grading wheels have three luma ranges with invisible pivots) — this panel is category-creating relative to LR, so "Lightroom or better" here means "Resolve-correct".

**What Lumen does.** Five zones + Global on the normalized tonal axis `x = (t − blackAnchorEV)/(whiteAnchorEV − blackAnchorEV)` (`ToneEngine.swift:377–381`); per-zone EV summed as `globalEV + Σ wᵢ·evᵢ` with raised-cosine partition-of-unity crossfades between adjacent pivots — pivots are zone *centres*, weight 1 at own pivot, 0 at neighbours' (`ZoneWeights.swift:11–15, 29–48, 62–66`; `ZonesPanel.swift:19–22`). Composes with the six sliders by summing stop fields into the same gain LUT through the same guided mask (`ToneEngine.swift:384–403`). Pivots stored normalized, count-validated against untrusted sidecars (`ToneEngine.swift:388–390`; `Recipe.swift:329–376`), draggable with a 0.02 minimum gap (`ZonesPanel.swift:124–146`). Deliberately **not** run through the monotonicity solver — it is the power tool; the bake's forward clamp is the only backstop (`ToneEngine.swift:410–424`). UI: EV dials −3…+3 soft / −5…+5 hard (`ZonesPanel.swift:71–83`).

**Verdict: DIVERGES from its own spec; core math CORRECT vs the field.** The stops-per-zone-over-an-edge-aware-mask model is exactly the documented Resolve/darktable consensus, and summing stop fields is the right composition (it's literally darktable's equalizer curve, evaluated at five movable points). Three concrete divergences:
1. ~~Defect (spec mismatch): default pivot placement~~ **FIXED** (5291500): `Zones.defaultPivots` is now DEFINED by the documented EVs (−4/−2/0/+2/+4 through the engine's own axis constants), locked by `testTheDefaultZonePivotsSitAtTheirDocumentedEVs`; the zones.* proof family was re-pinned from the sweep's own printout and PROOF-04 (Dark zone invisible) closed by measurement.
2. **Defect-by-omission: `falloff`, `wheel`, `sat` are wire-only.** The spec ships per-zone falloff (Resolve's signature affordance), a wheel, and saturation; no stage reads them — `zonePanelStops` consumes `.ev` alone and identity-detection ignores the rest (`ZonesPanel.swift:13–17`; `ToneEngine.swift:384–392, 470–473`). The panel honestly hides them, but "equal to Resolve's HDR palette" is currently false: it is darktable's equalizer at five points, not Resolve's palette.
3. **Unlimited by design:** opposing zone EVs can hit the bake clamp and posterize (flat LUT runs) rather than being eased like the six sliders. Deliberate and documented (:410–417), but unlike Resolve there is no UI signal when it happens.

**Gaps** (ranked):
1. Fix the default-pivot ↔ EV-denomination mismatch (either re-derive stored defaults from the documented EVs — −4 EV ⇒ x=0.357 etc. — or document the current EVs and change docs/04).
2. Implement per-zone `falloff` (the raised-cosine could take a per-boundary exponent/width) — it's the one Resolve affordance users will actually miss.
3. Zones-panel clamp telemetry: surface "this combination is flattening N% of the axis" the way `zonalScale`/`effectiveHighlights` surface easing for the sliders.
4. Per-zone wheel/sat: either implement against the shared grade engine (the code path exists — `GradeEngine`, shared pivots grammar) or strip from the wire format before it fossilizes.

## 9. The Curve (parametric + point)

**Expected math.**
- Adobe (documented, fetched — [image-tone-color.html](https://helpx.adobe.com/lightroom-classic/desktop/process-and-develop-photos/image-tone-color.html)): tone curve is display-referred over the familiar encoded axis; four region sliders (Highlights/Lights/Darks/Shadows) with **three draggable split controls**; "The Darks and Lights sliders affect mainly the middle region of the curve. The Highlight and Shadows sliders affect mainly the ends"; point curve with channel (RGB/R/G/B) editing, TAT. Point-curve interpolation is **undocumented**.
- Adobe *Refine Saturation* (documented): point-curve edits change both luminance and saturation by default (100 = legacy); "**Setting the slider to 0 restricts the effects of the Point Curve to only the luminance values**" — Lightroom June 2023 feature summary ([helpx.adobe.com/lightroom-cc/using/whats-new/2023-4.html](https://helpx.adobe.com/lightroom-cc/using/whats-new/2023-4.html)) and Matt Kloskowski's write-up ([mattk.com](https://mattk.com/curves-update-new-refine-saturation-adjustment-in-lightroom-and-photoshop/)) *(snippets)*.
- RawTherapee (documented, fetched — [Rawpedia Exposure](https://rawpedia.rawtherapee.com/Exposure)): parametric curve = four sliders + three control points, same regions; and the key attribution for LR's default color behavior: "**Film-Like**… This curve type was designed by Adobe as a part of DNG and **is thus the one used by Adobe Camera Raw and Lightroom**" — i.e. LR's default curve application is per-channel film-like (saturating with contrast).
- darktable (documented, fetched): curves are display-referred with explicit color-space/preserve-colors handling; RGB-linked-channels contrast "is known to desaturate highlights and boost saturation in the shadows, but this has proven to be the most reliable way" ([tone-curve](https://docs.darktable.org/usermanual/4.8/en/module-reference/processing-modules/tone-curve/) / [rgb-curve](https://docs.darktable.org/usermanual/4.8/en/module-reference/processing-modules/rgb-curve/) docs).
- Monotone point interpolation: Fritsch–Carlson monotone cubic is the standard cited method (F. N. Fritsch, R. E. Carlson, *Monotone Piecewise Cubic Interpolation*, SIAM J. Numer. Anal. 17(2), 1980 — classical reference; LR's own choice is **inferred unknown**).

**What Lumen does.**
- **Domain — matches consensus:** the curve runs post-display-transform on display-linear values through the sRGB-encoded axis, normalized by display white (`CurveStack.swift:2–6, 17–19, 267–271`; composed into the finish LUT at `RenderPlan.swift:193–217`; two-tap local-curve rationale `CurveStack.swift:340–355`).
- **Parametric:** four regions over movable splits (defaults 0.25/0.5/0.75, `Recipe.swift:460–474`; centres clamped `CurveStack.swift:59–65`), region weights = the same raised-cosine crossfade as the zones × an endpoint-pinning envelope `4x(1−x)` (`CurveStack.swift:125–139` — black and white points cannot move, asserted `EngineTests.swift:643`), per-region amplitude solved from the region's own shape to a slope floor of 0.2 (`CurveStack.swift:69–90, 162–166` — kills both the dead-top-of-travel and the reachable flat band), one combination scale solved closed-form to slope ≥0 with the shared 0.8 soft knee (`CurveStack.swift:107–118, 199–218` — adjacent regions opposed = an intentional 80:1 compression plateau, monotone, no flat sample), forward-max clamp on the stored grid because the probe and bake grids only meet at endpoints (`CurveStack.swift:92–105, 220–239`). Monotone at extremes asserted (`EngineTests.swift:613`).
- **Point curve:** Fritsch–Carlson monotone cubic, flat extension outside endpoints (`MonotoneCubic.swift:1–9, 40–60`); master = parametric ∘ point (`CurveStack.swift:245–249`); TAT and point-editing grammar (`CurveStack.swift:314–337`).
- **Color behavior — deliberate LR divergence:** default is luminance-preserving (curve the luminance, carry chroma ratios), i.e. **LR "Refine Saturation = 0" semantics as the default**, with legacy chroma amplification as opt-in; documented as such in the wire format (`Recipe.swift:421–424`; `CurveStack.swift:274–288`). Separate Luma curve (C1's tool) and per-channel R/G/B curves exist (`CurveStack.swift:290–301`). Hue stability of the preserving mode asserted (`EngineTests.swift:669`).

**Verdict: CORRECT on domain, structure, and monotonicity — better-founded than the LR behavior it clones; two deliberate, documented divergences.** (1) Default luminance preservation inverts LR's default (LR ships film-like/RefineSat=100 as legacy baggage — Adobe's 2023 addition of the 0 setting is documentary evidence the luminance-only behavior is the intentful one). Defensible, but an LR refugee's S-curve will *not* pump saturation the way their hands expect; the opt-in must be discoverable. (2) The parametric limiter (slope floor 0.2, eased combinations) means extreme settings produce *less* curve than LR's parametric can (LR's limits are undocumented; its parametric visibly flattens at extremes — inferred). Lumen trades peak authority for no-dead-travel and no-posterization, with the trade quantified in-code (30.6 vs 38.3 code values, `CurveStack.swift:85–89`). One UNPROVEN: LR's point-curve interpolant is unknown, so exact point-curve shape matching with an imported LR sidecar curve cannot be claimed — only monotonicity and pass-through-points (`EngineTests.swift:650`).

**Gaps** (ranked):
1. **Sidecar fidelity test:** import an LR XMP with a parametric + point curve (the sidecar path exists, `SidecarAndIngestTests.swift:404–443`) and golden the rendered delta; that is the only way to bound the unknown-interpolant risk.
2. Soft-clipped ends (Resolve-style per-end clip+softness) are spec'd in docs/04 and the wire comment (`Recipe.swift:421–423`) but not implemented — the matte-fade look currently requires point-stacking, exactly what the spec calls a hack.
3. The `Chroma boost` (legacy saturation) dial from docs/04 is likewise deferred; without it, "LR-default feel" for curves is unreachable, which contradicts the muscle-memory commitment. Ship the dial before shipping the claim.
4. Parametric splits: docs/04 says 10–90% draggable; code clamps to 0.02–0.98 (`CurveStack.swift:61–63`). Harmonize (cosmetic).
