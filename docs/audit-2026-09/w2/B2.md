**Method.** Every number below was recomputed in a from-scratch Python re-implementation
of `ZoneWindows`, `ZoneWeights.crossfade`, `Num.softKnee`, `solveLumScale`,
`solveBrillianceScale`, the OKLab Rec.2020 context and `LumenUCS.scaleBrightness`, sampled
over the whole −9 … +5 EV axis. The model reproduces the engine's published constants
exactly (neutral `L = Y^(1/3)` to 1e-15; default pivots at −4.38 / +0.38 EV).

**The brief's headline question — what keeps luminance monotone in dt that Lumen lacks.**
`r4-opensource.md:144-152`: dt's 4-way is an *offset* (global), a *masked multiplicative
gain ≥ 0* (shadows/highlights), and a *power about a fulcrum* (midtones/contrast), all in
**linear Yrg**; a power is monotone for any exponent > 0 and a non-negative gain never
crosses, so no limiter is needed and none exists. It also runs `Ych[0] = max(Ych[0], 0)`
plus `gamut_check_Yrg` **inside the module, always on**. Lumen instead applies zone
luminance as stops of perceptual **J** (`GradeEngine.swift:240-253`), where the 3× cube
leverage plus *additive* zone weights makes folding possible, and then has to bolt on two
numerical limiters to undo it — one of which is still wrong (B2-01) — and it deliberately
removed the in-module gamut guard (`:504-521`, `:892-909`), which is what lets B2-03
produce negative scene luminance. The structural answer is dt's: apply wheel Luminance and
Brilliance as a masked gain in linear light, and the limiters stop being needed at all.

## Findings

There is **no new S1 in this area** and I am not inflating one to fill the slot. K-044's
S1 half — the wheels — is genuinely fixed and I measured it. The strongest new findings are
two S2s: a limiter that switches itself off on the settings it exists for, and a grading
wheel whose face is up to 50° away from the colour it applies.

### B2-01 — the Brilliance limiter reports "nothing to limit" on the settings that fold hardest
severity: S2
confidence: measured
class: defect
size: S
evidence: `GradeEngine.swift:792-793`

```swift
let ceiling: Double = 64
guard !isMonotone(at: ceiling) else { return 1 }
```

`isMonotone` (`:776-789`) reads the gain through the floor at `:777`/`:779`
(`Num.safeLog2(Swift.max(1 + global + s * zoned[i], 0))`). Whenever any zone is pushed to
gain 0, a large trial `s` drives **every** sample to that floor, the sampled profile goes
flat, `isMonotone(64)` returns true, the guard fires and the limiter returns `1`. The
predicate is therefore not monotone in `s` — true for small `s`, false in the middle, true
again at the ceiling — which also invalidates the bisection at `:794-801`.

Measured, over shadows/mid/high ∈ {−100,−50,0,+50,+100} × Blending ∈ {0,25,50,75,100},
sampling the realised luminance `0.18·2^t·G(t)³` every 0.005 EV from −9 to +5:

| Brilliance sh / mid / high | Blending | `brillianceScale` | reversal |
|---|---|---|---|
| −50 / −50 / −100 | **50 (shipped)** | **1.0000** | brightest at −0.71 EV = 28.5 code; pure black by +1.88 EV → **28.5 code values** |
| −100 / −50 / −100 | 50 | 1.0000 | identical, 28.5 code values |
| −50 / −100 / 0 | 50 | 1.0000 | 1.5 code values, in the deep shadows |

**12 of 125 zone combinations invert, at every Blending including the default**, and the
engine reports `brillianceScale = 1.0` for all of them. The wheels' `solveLumScale` is
immune only because its stops are linear and have no floor.

`GradeLuminanceInversionTests.testBrillianceCannotInvertTheToneResponse` (`:143-166`) is
green because its roster is four tuples — `(100,0,0)`, `(0,0,−100)`, `(100,−60,100)`,
`(0,100,−100)` — none of which drives two zones negative, and all four at the default
Blending only. Its sibling `testOpposedWheelsAreMonotoneAtEveryBlending` (`:68-88`) does
sweep Blending; the Brilliance test does not.

how the owner sees it: he pulls Brilliance down on the shadows and the midtones and hard
down on the highlights to crush a scene, and the highlights come back **pure black** while
the midtones still render — a black band across the top of the tone scale, with the
brightest part of the picture the darkest thing in it. Nothing in the panel warns him and
the engine's own guard says there was nothing to limit.

fix: `GradeEngine.swift:792-793` — delete the ceiling guard's shortcut and replace the
bisection with a scan that takes the **smallest** `s` at which `isMonotone` first fails
(or, better, exclude floored samples from the slope test: a run of consecutive zero-gain
samples is a legitimate crush, but the *transition into and out of* it is not). Test that
goes red today: extend `testBrillianceCannotInvertTheToneResponse`'s roster with
`(-50, -50, -100)` and `(-50, -100, 0)` and loop it over `[0, 25, 50, 75, 100]` Blending —
the first tuple fails at t = −0.71 EV at the default Blending, luminance 0.01197 falling to
0. Add `XCTAssertLessThan(engine.colorBalance.brillianceScale, 1.0)` for that tuple, which
also pins the guard rather than only the symptom.

### B2-02 — the grading wheel draws SwiftUI HSB and the engine applies OKLab: mean 29.6°, worst 50.4° of hue error
severity: S2
confidence: measured
class: defect
size: S
evidence: `LumenControls.swift:1901-1903`

```swift
static let wheelColors: [Color] = (0..<13).map {
    Color(hue: Double($0) / 12, saturation: 0.72, brightness: 0.8)
}
```

painted as `AngularGradient(colors: Self.wheelColors, center: .center)` (`:1767`), with the
puck at `offset(x: r * cos(a), y: r * sin(a))` (`:1888`) and the drag reading
`hue = atan2(dy, dx)` (`:1817`) — all three on the same clockwise-from-3-o'clock
convention, so the puck sits exactly on the drawn colour. The engine then reads that same
number as an **OKLab ab angle**: `WheelTint.init:215-217`,
`a = 0.15·sat·cos(hue), b = 0.15·sat·sin(hue)`.

Measured — the tint the engine lays on mid-grey, converted Rec.2020 → sRGB and read back as
an HSB hue, against the HSB hue drawn under the puck at the same angle:

| puck at (drawn) | grade actually applies | error |
|---|---|---|
| 60° orange-yellow | 31° orange | −29° |
| 120° **green** | 70° **yellow** | −50° |
| 240° **blue** | 201° **cyan** | −39° |
| 300° magenta | 264° violet | −36° |

**mean |error| 29.6°, max 50.4°** over 24 sampled angles. And the fix already exists one
file over: `ColorPanel.hueColor` (`ColorPanel.swift:958-980`) does exactly the right thing
and its own comment says why — *"evaluated through OKLCh and the working space rather than
through SwiftUI's HSB, whose hue angle is a different number entirely. A ring that put
'29°' somewhere other than where the engine's red band sits would be a diagram of a
different tool."* `wheelColors`' own comment (`:1891-1900`) claims the mixer's ring and this
wheel "read as siblings"; they do not — one is a diagram of the engine and one is a
diagram of SwiftUI.

how the owner sees it: he drags the puck into the blue of the shadows wheel and the
shadows come out cyan; he drags into the green and gets yellow. There is no numeric H/S
readout on this wheel (`LookPanel.swift:550` passes `title: ""`, and the spec's numeric
entry is not built), so nothing on screen can tell him the number he set is not the colour
he picked.

fix: `LumenControls.swift:1901-1903` — build `wheelColors` through `ColorPanel.hueColor`
(or lift that function into `LumenControls`/`Lumen` so both instruments share it), at the
same L and a chroma that matches `GradeEngine.maxABOffset`; sample it at ≥ 24 stops, not
13, so the ring stays smooth once the spacing stops being uniform in HSB. Test that goes
red today: `LumenAppTests` (or a LumenCore helper test on the shared function)
`testTheGradingWheelPaintsTheHueItApplies` — for each of 24 angles, convert
`OKLCh(L: 0.72, C: 0.16, h: θ)` to sRGB and assert its hue is within 5° of the wheel's
drawn colour at θ; today 120° is off by 50.4°. Note K-102: no CI lane runs `LumenAppTests`
under a filter, so the LumenCore-side placement is the one that will actually run.

### B2-03 — "L is not touched, so tinting a zone carries no luminance contamination" is false by up to 0.15 EV, and drives scene luminance negative in the shadows
severity: S2
confidence: measured
class: defect
size: M
evidence: `GradeEngine.swift:9-11` (the file header) —

```
//   · a wheel tint is a CONSTANT-LUMINANCE hue/chroma offset — a translation of the
//     OKLab (a, b) pair, weighted by the zone windows. L is not touched, so tinting a
//     zone carries no luminance contamination and a wheel at sat = 0 is an exact no-op.
```

and the code it describes, `:479-486` (`lab.a += da; lab.b += db`). Holding OKLab **L** is
not holding luminance: `Y` is only `L³` on the neutral axis, and an ab translation leaves
it. Measured on a neutral through this engine's own Rec.2020 OKLab context, Global wheel
(weight 1 everywhere) at sat = 1, `|ab| = 0.15`:

| wheel hue | ΔEV of luminance at mid-grey |
|---|---|
| 0° | −0.152 |
| 180° | **+0.141** |
| 330° | −0.148 |

so the two ends of the ring differ by **0.29 EV — about 12 sRGB code values at mid-grey —
of pure luminance, from a control the header says cannot move luminance at all**. Because
the offset is *absolute* rather than proportional to the pixel's lightness, it gets worse
as the picture gets darker: at sat 0.50 the worst case over the axis is **−1.57 EV** (at
−9 EV, hue 285°), and at **sat ≥ 0.75 the output luminance goes negative** (first at
−8.25 EV, hue 295°). `apply` only guards `out.isFinite` (`:503`), so negative scene-linear
RGB leaves the stage; the module's own gamut guard was deliberately removed (`:504-521`),
which is precisely the guard dt keeps always-on inside `color balance rgb`
(`r4-opensource.md:139`, `Ych[0] = max(Ych[0], 0)` + `gamut_check_Yrg`) — and docs/05:196-197
promises Lumen has it too: *"soft gamut clipping at constant hue is permanently on"*.

`solveLumScale` also does not see this: it samples only the Luminance rings. Measured with
the rings at zero and a Shadows-only tint at sat 1, Blending 0, the tint alone folds the
response by **−0.0167 EV per 0.0035 EV step** at the crossfade.

how the owner sees it: two wheels at the same saturation, opposite hues, are supposed to be
two different colours of the same strength; one of them makes the zone visibly brighter and
the other visibly darker. With the Shadows wheel pushed hard, deep shadows stop being
darker-than-black and start being *negative*, which is a different picture at every
downstream stage.

fix: `GradeEngine.swift:479-486` — after the ab translation, restore the pixel's input
luminance (or its H-K brightness J, which is the same operation the file already owns in
`LumenUCS.scaleBrightness`) before the Luminance move; that makes the header's claim true
and removes the negative excursion at the same time. Alternatively scale `maxABOffset` by
the pixel's L, dt's shape. Either way `docs/05:196-197`'s "permanently on" clip needs to
either exist here or be struck from the spec. Test that goes red today:
`GradeLuminanceInversionTests.testAWheelTintDoesNotMoveLuminance` — for
`hue ∈ {0, 90, 180, 270}`, `sat = 1`, Global wheel, assert
`|log2(Y_out/Y_in)| < 0.01` at t ∈ {−6, −3, 0, +2}; today hue 180 at t = 0 returns
**+0.141 EV** and hue 295 at sat 1 returns a negative `Y_out`.

### B2-04 — at Blending 0 the Luminance rings and the whole Brilliance grid are inert, and the two tests that claim otherwise assert a 1e-9 threshold

confidence: measured
class: defect
size: M

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **0** | 0.02018 | 0.02023 | 0.02023 | 0.02023 | 0.02023 |
| 25 | 0.05000 | 0.12500 | 0.24956 | 0.29329 | 0.30064 |
| 50 | 0.05000 | 0.12500 | 0.25000 | 0.37500 | 0.49899 |

At Blending 0 the ring's whole travel from 0.25 to 1.00 differs by **4.4e-6 stops**. Same
shape for Brilliance Highlights (effective value out of 100, after `brillianceScale`):

| Blending | −5 | −10 | −20 | −50 | −100 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **0** | **1.39** | **1.39** | **1.39** | **1.39** | **1.39** |
| 25 | 5.00 | 10.00 | 17.67 | 18.88 | 18.91 |
| 50 | 5.00 | 10.00 | 20.00 | 33.47 | 34.04 |

Two consequences. (a) At Blending 0 both controls are dead over 95 % of their travel.
(b) `GradeEngine.swift:743-746` claims *"below the knee ordinary settings (the panel's
documented ±20 working range included) are exactly unlimited"* — measured, that holds only
for **Blending ≥ 35**: at Blending 25 a lone Brilliance −20 is delivered as 17.67, at

The tests certify the wrong property. `testTheLuminanceRingKeepsDoingMoreUnderTheCorrectedSolve`

asserts `> previousGain + 1e-9` and never leaves Blending 50. Both would still pass if the
softKnee were replaced by any strictly-increasing but visually flat function.

how the owner sees it: he drags Blending to 0 to get a hard zone separation — the reason the
control exists — and from there the Luminance ring and every Brilliance slider stop
responding. He can move Brilliance Highlights from −5 to −100 and the picture does not
change by one code value.

a stop — it is an honesty bug of the same class as the tone panel's `effectiveHighlights`
(`ToneEngine.swift:110-114`). Surface the realised strength: publish
`GradeEngine.lumScale` and `ColorBalanceGrid.brillianceScale` to the panel and have the
Luminance caption and the Brilliance note (`LookPanel.swift:394-399`, drawn at `:525`) and the wheel's
Luminance caption (`LumenControls.swift:1851-1856`) read out the
applied value when the limiter binds, exactly as the Highlights slider does. Test that goes
red today: `testTheLuminanceRingIsNotDeadAtHardBlending` — assert the realised peak stops at
`lum = 1.0` are at least 5× those at `lum = 0.25` for every Blending in `[0, 10, 25, 50]`;
today Blending 0 gives a ratio of **1.0000022**. Then the same for Brilliance at Blending 0
(5 vs 100 both realise 1.39).

### B2-06 — the wheels ship without the interaction grammar the spec pins as LrC parity, and the printer lights spend the spec's coarse/fine modifiers on channel select
severity: S3
confidence: traced
class: parity-gap
size: M
evidence: `docs/05:180` requires *"Modifier keys | Cmd = hue-only, Shift = sat-only, Alt =
fine | LrC-compatible"*, `:174` *"numeric H/S entry behind disclosure triangle"*, `:179`
*"Zone falloff (per pivot) | 0..1 | 0.5"*, `:181` *"Mask preview (per zone) | toggle"*,
`:188` *"Opponent picker (per wheel) | eyedropper"*, `:208` *"Keyboard: wheel focus 1–4,
arrows nudge hue, Shift+arrows saturation"*. Against the code:
`LumenColorWheel` (`LumenControls.swift:1763-1830`) has one `DragGesture` and no
`NSApp.currentEvent` modifier read, no `focusable`, no `keyboardShortcut`, and no numeric
field; `LookPanel.wheel` passes `title: ""` (`:550`) so there is no label to attach one to.
`ZoneWindows` has `shadowHalfWidth`/`highlightHalfWidth` held separately *"so the per-pivot
`falloffs` field the format is due to gain can drive them independently"*
(`GradeEngine.swift:76-78`) — the field does not exist on `GradingWheels`
(`RecipeLook.swift:229-244`), so both are always equal. `ControlIndex.swift:146-152` lists
the three sections and no per-wheel control. Confirmed by the gap table's own count
(`gap-table.md` §B, *"0 hits for wheel modifier keys"*).

The printer lights are the sharper half: `docs/05:235` allots `⇧` to a 4-point coarse step
and `⌥` to a quarter-point fine step, but `LookPanel.swift:628-641` spends `⌃` on Red, `⌥`
