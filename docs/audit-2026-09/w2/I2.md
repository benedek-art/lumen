# W2 — I2 · Kernels vs the reference

**The question.** Per kernel: does the GPU compute what the CPU reference computes?
Precision (K-050), clamping (K-048), edge handling, ROI for the general kernels,
N-003's halation mass, TEXSPEC's 0.74 at 3 px, and which goldens have tolerances loose
enough to hide a defect.

**Files read, every line.** `Sources/LumenPipeline/Kernels.swift` (all 38 kernel
sources and the whole registry) · `Sources/LumenCore/Engine/ReferenceRenderer.swift` ·
`Sources/LumenCore/Image/ImageBuffer.swift` ·
`Tests/LumenPipelineTests/KernelGoldenTests.swift`.

**Read to trace the other end of each kernel (not owned, not audited):**
`RenderGraph.swift` (`applyTone` 568–592, `applyPresence`/`applyDetailBands` 596–758,
`applyDehaze` 760–898, `applyLocal` 902–930, `applySharpen` 1036–1110, `gaussianBlur`
1127–1163, vignette 1224–1325, `applyHalation` 1332–1373, `grainPlate` 1430–1505,
`logLuminance` 1547–1557, `boxBlur`/`guidedFilter` 1570–1674, ROI helpers 468–520) ·
`FilmLab.swift` (`HalationProfile` 441–580, `FilmGrainProfile` 580–1180) ·
`DetailEngine.swift` (`Decomposition` 148–180, `applyTexture` 285–309, vignette
606–818) · `SpatialOps.swift` (`guidedFilter` 246–341, `atrousWavelet` 349–370) ·
`MaskRaster.swift` (`linearPlane` 508–530, `radialPlane` 594–648) · `MaskGPU.swift` ·

## Known-open dispositions

- **K-048** (GPU log-luminance plane has no floor) — **FIXED-SINCE, verified.**
  `RenderGraph.logLuminance` (`RenderGraph.swift:1547–1557`) now floors before the
  encode, through `highlightEnergy(threshold: 0, boost: 1)` — literally `max(v, 0)` per
  channel — matching `ReferenceRenderer.applyTone`'s `LumenLog.encode(max(lum, 0))`
  (`ReferenceRenderer.swift:189`). Both tone-mask call sites go through it
  (`RenderGraph.swift:569`, `:613`, `:865`), and `testLogLuminanceFloorsANegativeLuminancePixelAtZero`
  (`KernelGoldenTests.swift:3098`) holds it. I also checked the encode kernel itself
  against the Swift twin: `lumenLogEncode` guards its log branch with `max(c, 1e-9)` and
  selects branchlessly with `step(linearCut, c)` (`LUT.swift:67–77`), so a negative
  input cannot produce a NaN that `mix` would propagate. Correct.

- **K-050** (every spatial stage runs on an fp16 log plane) — **STILL-OPEN, and now
  measured.** The working format is `CIFormat.RGBAh` (`PipelineRenderer.swift:78`,
  `DecodeMaterializer.swift:94`), so `meanI` and `meanII` in
  `RenderGraph.guidedFilter` (`:1661–1673`) are fp16 textures and
  `lumenGuidedCoefficients` forms `meanII − meanI²` — a cancellation of two numbers
  near 0.25 whose fp16 quantum is 1.22e-4, against a presence ε of
  `baseEpsilon / range² = 1.736e-5`. **The quantisation error in the variance is ~7× ε.**
  Measured by re-deriving the whole filter in Python on a ramp + Nyquist-texture + 3 EV
  step frame, f64 against fp16-at-every-texture-boundary, at the presence fine radius
  (8): the detail band's σ over the flat region goes **0.02670 EV → 0.04509 EV (1.69×)**
  and the worst per-pixel band error over the frame is **0.137 EV**. The tone mask's ε is
  fourteen times larger (`(0.375/24)² = 2.44e-4`) and is unaffected — measured band σ
  identical to five figures. So the row is right, it is presence-specific, and the fix
  is the denomination change it names: run the presence guide on an EV plane (where ε is
  0.01 and the cancellation is 0.01-relative, not 1.7e-5-relative) or compute the
  variance as `box((I − meanI)²)` so no cancellation happens. The reference is not
  exposed: `Plane` stores f32 (`ImageBuffer.swift:172`), where the same cancellation is
  0.2% of ε.

- **N-001** (three mask kernels dead on reserved words) — **FIXED-SINCE, with one
  residue.** `long` → `edge` and `out` → `folded` are gone (`Kernels.swift:497–553`);
  `testEveryKernelCompiles` now asserts `parametricMasksAvailable` as well as
  `isAvailable` (`KernelGoldenTests.swift:1443–1457`); `check-swift-surface.py` pass 13
  runs and reports **"38 kernel sources declare no reserved-word identifier"** — I ran
  it, and 38 is exactly the number of `make`/`makeGeneral` calls in the file, so the
  pass sees every kernel. I also re-derived both mask kernels against
  `MaskRaster.linearPlane` and `radialPlane` term by term (long-edge units, the
  `h − (destCoord().y − oy)` row flip, the pixel-centre convention, the `rin` guard, the
  `1 − smoothstep(rin, 1, r)` direction): **they agree exactly.**
  The residue is the roster, not the words — see **I2-01**.

- **N-003** (halation mass gpu 6.4517 vs reference 6.8944) — **CONFIRMED and
  EXPLAINED, exactly.** It is not a bug in the blur; it is the reconstruction ramp.
  `lumenHighlightEnergy` is `max(E − 0.25, 0) × 2^0.3` (`Kernels.swift:433–438`);
  `HalationProfile.highlightEnergy` is `t·E·2^(0.3t)` with `t = smoothstep(−4, 0, log2 E)`
  (`FilmLab.swift:541–552`). At the golden's 4.0 highlight the ratio is
  `(4 − 0.25)/4 = **0.9375**`; the recorded 6.4517/6.8944 = 0.9358, which is that number
  plus the f32 read-back and the block's own rim. Two paths, one documented
  approximation (`FilmLab.swift:562–566`) — but the approximation is much worse than 6%
  everywhere except the case the golden picked. See **I2-02**.

- **N-005** (three dye-layer plate seeds correlate at r ≈ 0.088) — **REFUTED, measured.**
  I ported `latticeValue` and `plate` to Python and reproduced the row's numbers exactly:
  r01 = **0.0880**, r02 = 0.0459, r12 = −0.0376 on the shipped seeds. Then I built the
  null: 200 pairs of *unrelated* random seeds give mean −0.0035 and **sd 0.1104**, |r| at
  the 95th percentile 0.212, max 0.303. The shipped values sit at the **57th, 31st and
  24th percentile** of |r| for seeds with nothing in common. The row's "over 16 384
  samples" is the error: a 128×128 plate is not 16 384 independent samples. Its lowest
  octave is an 8×8 lattice at amplitude 1 (`FilmLab.swift:1073`, `baseFrequency = n/16`),
  smoothstep-interpolated, so the effective degrees of freedom is ~64 and the null sd of
  r is 0.11 — not 1/√16384 = 0.0078. `plateSeed` decorrelates as well as any seed pair
  can. Nothing to fix. (What is *worth* keeping from the row: the fact that nobody could
  tell — there is no test that asks this question. `testGrainPlateCarriesThreeLayersOnAColourStock`
  bounds |r| < 0.6, which is 5.4 null-sd wide.)

- **N-006** (`HalationProfile.normalizedWeights` is unused) — **CONFIRMED.**
  `grep -rn "normalizedWeights" Sources/ Tests/` returns exactly one hit, its own
  declaration at `FilmLab.swift:525`. Both stages accumulate the raw weights —
  `ReferenceRenderer.swift:477–487` and `RenderGraph.swift:1344–1366` each start
  `weight = 1.0` and do `weight *= profile.decay`, summing 1 + 0.5 + 0.25 = **1.75**. The
  two paths agree, so it is a calibration question, not a parity break, exactly as the
  disposition says. Amount 100 delivers 1.75× the energy the profile's `strength`
  nominally means, and `strength` is a *measured* per-stock quantity
  (`FilmLab.swift:244` etc.), so the calibration is off by a constant that the stock
  numbers have absorbed. Fixing it changes every stock's halo by 1.75× and wants the
  strengths re-derived in the same commit.

**Cross-cutting rows** (K-014, K-058, K-073, K-102): not touched; no disposition.

## Findings

### I2-01 — `blendMaskMode` is in no availability roster, so if it ever fails to compile every Luminosity and Colour mask is silently dropped and the sentinel stays green
severity: S2
confidence: measured
class: defect
size: S
evidence: `Kernels.swift` compiles 38 kernels (`grep -c 'public static let \w* = make'`).
`unavailableKernels` (`Kernels.swift:944–979`) lists **33**; `unavailableMaskKernels`
(`:987–994`) lists **4**. Cross-checked mechanically:

```
compiled: 38   unavailableKernels: 33   unavailableMaskKernels: 4
MISSING FROM EVERY ROSTER: ['blendMaskMode']
```

`KernelLibrary.isAvailable` is `unavailableKernels.isEmpty` (`:940`), so a nil
`blendMaskMode` leaves it **true**, and `testEveryKernelCompiles`
(`KernelGoldenTests.swift:1443–1457`) — which asserts exactly those two rosters — passes.
The consequence is worse than N-001's, because there is no fallback:
`RenderGraph.applyLocal` (`RenderGraph.swift:921–927`) does

```swift
composite = KernelLibrary.apply(KernelLibrary.blendMaskMode, extent: out.extent, …)
…
guard let blended = composite else { continue }
```

`continue` drops **the whole mask** — its exposure, its curve, its colour — not just its
blend mode. And the kernel has no test of any kind: `grep -rn blendMaskMode Tests/`
returns nothing. The file header's own count is the tell: `Kernels.swift:2` says
"thirty-three small kernels", which is the size of the roster that is missing one, not
the size of the registry.
how the owner sees it: on a machine or an OS release where this one shader does not
compile, a mask set to Luminosity or Colour disappears from the picture entirely — the
adjustment is in the panel, the mask overlay draws, and no pixel moves — while the
"Reduced — N GPU kernels unavailable" note in the HUD (`RenderCoordinator.swift:305–310`)
lists nothing, because it prints `unavailableKernels`. I verified the kernel's arithmetic
against `MaskAlgebra.blended` (`MaskAlgebra.swift:53–75`) line by line and it is correct
today; this is about what happens when it is not, which is the whole of N-001's story.
fix: add `("blendMaskMode", blendMaskMode)` to `unavailableKernels` (it belongs there
rather than in the mask roster: the mask roster exists because a parametric-mask kernel
has an honest `MaskRaster` fallback, and this one has none), correct the header count to
38, and — the durable half — add a pass to `scripts/check-swift-surface.py` beside pass
13 that parses `Kernels.swift` for `public static let X = make…` and fails when `X`
appears in neither roster. That check is nine lines and is the mechanical form of the
lesson N-001 taught. **The test that goes red today:** the new checker pass, run against
the file as it stands; and in the suite, a `KernelGoldenTests` case
`testBlendMaskModeMatchesMaskAlgebra` that renders a Luminosity and a Colour composite
through the kernel and compares against `MaskAlgebra.blended` — it cannot even be written
today without adding the kernel to a roster to skip on.

### I2-02 — Halation's GPU reconstruction is a pedestal where the reference is a smoothstep, and the divergence is 6% only at the highlight level the one test happens to use
severity: S2
confidence: measured
class: parity-gap
size: S
evidence: `Kernels.swift:433–438` — `e = max(image.rgb − threshold, 0) · boost` — against
`FilmLab.swift:541–552`:

```swift
let ev = log2(e / clipLevel)
let t  = Num.smoothstep(-HalationProfile.protectEV, 0, ev)   // protectEV = 4
out[i] = t * e * pow(2.0, HalationProfile.boostRange * t)    // boostRange = 0.3
```

with `threshold = clip · 2^-2 = 0.25` and `boost = 2^0.3` (`FilmLab.swift:569–576`).
Re-derived both in Python; the ratio is not a constant, it is `1 − 0.25/E` above the clip
and a cliff below it:

| scene-linear E | stops vs clip | reference | GPU | gpu/ref |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.125 | −3.0 | 0.0202 | **0.0000** | **0.00** |
| 0.25 | −2.0 | 0.1387 | **0.0000** | **0.00** |
| 0.50 | −1.0 | 0.5028 | 0.3078 | **0.61** |
| 1.00 | 0.0 (the clip) | 1.2311 | 0.9234 | **0.75** |
| 2.00 | +1.0 | 2.4623 | 2.1545 | 0.87 |
| **4.00** | **+2.0** | **4.9246** | **4.6168** | **0.9375** |
| 16.0 | +4.0 | 19.698 | 19.391 | 0.98 |

`testHalationGlowIsAsWideAsTheProfileSays` (`KernelGoldenTests.swift:824–933`) puts a
4.0 block on a 0.02 ground — the single row of that table where the two paths are closest
— and bounds the mass at `accuracy: referenceGlow.mass * 0.30` (`:919`). Applying the
table to that frame predicts gpu/ref = 0.93750; the recorded run is 6.4517/6.8944 =
0.93580. So the golden measures the best case and allows five times the error it finds.
A frame whose highlights sit just over the clip — a white shirt, an overcast sky, a
window at +0.1 stop, which is what most clipping in a photograph actually is — diverges by
**24%** and still passes; the whole protected shoulder, four stops of it, contributes
glow on the reference and **exactly none** on the GPU.
how the owner sees it: halation is weaker in the app and in every exported file than the
Film Lab number promises, and the deficit is largest on soft highlights — the ones where
the halo is the effect — and smallest on speculars, where it is least visible. A headless
render or a kernels-unavailable machine produces a *different, stronger* halo from the
same recipe.
fix: make the kernel the twin instead of a stand-in. `lumenHighlightEnergy` gains three
lines and one `log2` in a pass that already runs once per frame over the same pixels:
```
vec3 ev = log2(max(image.rgb, vec3(1e-9)) / vec3(clip));
vec3 t  = smoothstep(vec3(-protectEV), vec3(0.0), ev);
return vec4(t * image.rgb * exp2(vec3(boostRange) * t), 1.0);
```
passing `clipLevel`, `protectEV` and `boostRange` instead of the two derived scalars, and
deleting `HalationProfile.threshold`/`.boost` so there is no second reconstruction to keep
in step. **The test that goes red today:** in
`testHalationGlowIsAsWideAsTheProfileSays`, run the same measurement a second time with
the block at `1.05` instead of `4.0` and tighten the mass bar to 2% — measured, today's
ratio there is **0.762**, so it fails by a factor of twelve on the bar and would also fail
the existing 30% bar. Keep the 4.0 case as the wide-glow check.

### I2-03 — The only reference↔GPU spatial comparison in the repository has a bar of 0.25 of full scale, was written to be tightened after the first green run, and prints no number to tighten it to
severity: S2
confidence: measured
class: defect
size: S
evidence: `KernelGoldenTests.swift:1918` —
`XCTAssertLessThan(worst, 0.25, "the spatial path diverged by \(worst) at \(worstAt)")`
— on display-referred output in 0…1, i.e. **64 sRGB code values** per channel per pixel.
The test's own docstring (`:1855–1861`) says so: *"The tolerance is a SMOKE-TEST BOUND,
not a measurement… 0.25 is set to catch a stage wired to the wrong input, applied twice,
or missing entirely… Tighten it to the measured value on the first green run."* There have
been many green runs — `w0/perf-baseline.md` records a whole gpu-parity lane at 3m52s —
and the bound has not moved, because **the test never prints `worst`**: the number is only
in the assertion message, which XCTest emits on failure only. There is nothing in any log
to tighten it to. Compare the sibling colour-path golden (`:1817–1819`), which does the
job properly: bound 0.035, measured 0.028001785278320312, and a comment explaining that
the *stability* of the digits is what guards it. The Clarity gate in the same test
(`:2005`) is one-sided — `XCTAssertGreaterThan(clarity / referenceClarity, 0.2)` — so the
GPU may apply anywhere from a fifth to an unbounded multiple of the reference's Clarity;
`lumenDetailRemap`'s own comment records the measured window as 0.5×–1.3×
(`Kernels.swift:373–376`), so a regression to 3× — the crunchy over-processed direction
this stage was rewritten to escape — is invisible.
how the owner sees it: nothing, until a spatial kernel drifts. That is the point: this is
the one assertion standing between "the GPU implements the reference" and "the GPU does
something in the same neighbourhood", and at 64 code values it certifies almost nothing.
fix: `print` the measured `worst` (and the Clarity ratio) in the PerfProbe house style so
the next lane run records them, then set the bound from that number with the colour-path
golden's reasoning written in; add an upper gate `clarity / referenceClarity < 2.0`.
**The test that goes red today:** substitute any single spatial kernel's constant — set
`lumenDetailGain`'s clamp to ±0.4 instead of ±4, or drop the `perStop = LumenLog.range`
conversion at `RenderGraph.swift:676` — and this test stays **green** at 0.25 while the
picture changes by a factor of 24. That substitution is the proof, and it is the exact
defect the file records as having shipped once already (`:1935–1938`).

### I2-04 — Grain has no GPU↔reference comparison anywhere, while three source comments state that gpu-parity is what holds the two implementations together
severity: S2
confidence: measured
class: defect
size: M
evidence: `Kernels.swift:614–615` — *"this line changes pixels — deliberately, and
identically in `ReferenceRenderer.applyGrain`, **which is what gpu-parity checks**"* —
and `ReferenceRenderer.swift:544` and `:564`, both *"gpu-parity is what holds them
together"*, about `plateScale` and about `noiseLumaWeight`/`noiseOwnWeight`. It does not.
The roster of grain tests in `Tests/LumenPipelineTests`, in full:
`testGrainPlateCarriesThreeLayersOnAColourStock` (`:119`) and
`testGrainPlateIsGreyOnAMonochromeStock` (`:148`) assert properties of the **plate**, not
of an applied pixel, and never construct a reference render;
`testGrainIsAppliedOnTheGridThatIsDelivered` (`:1213`) compares **two GPU exports** to
each other (`:1282`, `rms < 0.15σ`); `testTheGamutFlagIsGrainedLikeTheReference` (`:3432`)
— named for the reference — asserts only that the flagged region varies by more than 0.002
and that its mean is near the warning colour (`:3499`, `:3504`), and calls
`ReferenceRenderer` nowhere. Of the three whole-graph parity goldens
(`testGraphMatchesTheReferenceRendererOnTheColourPath:1777`,
`…WithTheSpatialStagesOn:1867`, `ToneShippingGoldenTests`), **none sets
`recipe.look.filmLab`** — so grain, halation and the entire film chain sit outside every
comparison between the two renderers.
What that leaves unchecked is not hypothetical: the two implementations sample the plate
by different machinery. The reference calls
`FilmGrainProfile.sample(plates[c], x: Double(x)/scales[c], y: Double(y)/scales[c])`
with `x`,`y` **top-down** (`ReferenceRenderer.swift:571–576`); the GPU builds a top-down
RGBAf bitmap, hands it to `CIImage(bitmapData:)`, scales it by an affine transform and
tiles it with `CIFilter.affineTile` in Core Image's **bottom-up** space
(`RenderGraph.swift:1451–1465`). That is the same row-order crossing that produced
N-001's vertically mirrored mask alpha, on a signal — noise — where a mirror is invisible
to every eye and to every σ-based assertion, and where the non-integer tile period
(Portra's red cell is 0.6827 px, so the tile is 87.4 px) additionally has no reason to be
wrap-continuous the way `sample`'s modulo is.
how the owner sees it: a photograph rendered headless or on a kernels-unavailable machine
carries a different grain field from the same recipe on the same photograph in the app —
not a different *amount*, a different field. And the three landings this area has already
made on grain (the seed, the encode scale, C2-02's chroma mix) each rested on a parity
claim that no test makes.
fix: one golden, `testGrainMatchesTheReferenceRenderer` in `KernelGoldenTests` — a flat
0.18 frame, Portra 400 at Amount 100, rendered through `RenderGraph.build` with a plate
and through `ReferenceRenderer.render` on the same plan and seed, compared pixel for
pixel. **It goes red today** if the row order or the tile phase disagrees, which is the
question; if it goes green, the three comments become true and the next grain landing has
the harness they already claim. Second, add `recipe.look.filmLab = FilmLab(stock:
"lumen/portra400", amount: 100, halation: 100, grain: FilmGrain(size: 1, amount: 100))`
to `testGraphMatchesTheReferenceRendererWithTheSpatialStagesOn` so the film chain is
inside the whole-graph bar at all (with I2-03's bound tightened first, or it proves
nothing).

## Checked, and found correct — so nobody re-checks it

- **ROI is right on all four general kernels.** `lumenBSpline5` samples `p ± 2·tap` and
  is applied with `reach: 2·step` on each axis (`RenderGraph.swift:483–493`);
  `lumenBox3` samples `p ± tap` with `reach: 1` (`:503–510`); `lumenEdgeMap` and
  `lumenHotPixel` sample `p ± 1` with `reach: 1` (`:513`, `:470`). `applyGenerator`'s
  `{ _, rect in rect }` is correct for the two mask kernels, which read no input at all.
  Every neighbourhood call passes `clamped(image)` first, matching `Plane.clampedSample`.
  The `#if os(macOS)` split means none of this compiles here; it is read, not run.
- **The two parametric mask kernels are term-for-term the reference.** Re-derived
  `lumenMaskLinear` against `MaskRaster.linearPlane:508–530` and `lumenMaskRadial`

  comma-separated declarations (`float a = 1.0, b = 2.0;` — only `a` is checked), which no

## One-line verdict

There is **no S1** here: every kernel I re-derived computes what its reference computes,
and the two that were dead are alive and exact. What this area has instead is a
**measurement problem** — the one spatial parity bar is 64 code values wide and prints no
number, grain has no parity test at all while three comments say it does, the halation
golden picks the highlight level where the approximation is closest and allows five times
the error it finds, C2-01b substitutes back green, and one kernel is in no roster at all.
Four of the five findings are the same defect wearing different clothes: **the harness
asserts less than its own prose claims.** N-005 is refuted with a null distribution;
N-006 is confirmed and is a calibration decision, not a break.
