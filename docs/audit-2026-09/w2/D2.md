### K-090 — Dehaze sky guard reference-only: **STILL-OPEN, and now measured**

The floor is still flat on the GPU. `RenderGraph.swift:880` —
`let floorT = Num.mix(0.55, 0.05, distance)` — a single scalar handed to the kernel;
`Kernels.swift:152` — `float t = max(raw, floorT);`; the kernel's own note at
`Kernels.swift:143-146` still says "Still missing versus the reference: the sky guard…
It needs the gradient and log-luminance planes this kernel is not given." The reference
lifts the floor per pixel at `DetailEngine.swift:418-420`:
`floorT = mix(tMin, 0.9, saturate(bright · flat))`.

BUILDING.md called the divergence "slight". It is not. Measured on the proof standard's
own `hazySky` frame (`Tests/LumenCoreTests/Proof/ProofFrames.swift:338-355`, airlight
(0.55, 0.62, 0.78), true transmission `0.25 + 0.65·v`), reproducing both recombinations
exactly:

| Dehaze | sky mean ΔEV (GPU darker) | sky peak ΔEV | mean gain ref → gpu | approx sRGB code values, mean / peak |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| +100 | **0.691** | **1.135** | 0.720 → 0.458 | 28.7 / 41.7 |
| +50 | 0.240 | 0.340 | 0.860 → 0.729 | 11.7 / 15.6 |
| +25 | 0.106 | 0.143 | 0.930 → 0.865 | — |

The ground half of the frame is **bit-identical** (mean and peak ΔEV = 0.0000): the whole
divergence is in the sky, which is the only reason anyone drags Dehaze. The reference
floor exceeds the GPU's on **97.4%** of the sky pixels; the GPU floor binds (i.e. the raw
transmission is below 0.45) on **70.1%** of them. The code-value column is a plain sRGB
OETF on working luminance, not the shipped display transform — it is an order-of-magnitude
statement, the EV column is the exact one.

**Second half of the row (the negative branch) — STILL-OPEN in code, but inert on this
frame.** `Kernels.swift:167` is `float distant = clamp(1.0 - raw, 0.0, 1.0);` against the
reference's `Swift.max(1 - t[x, y], skyness)` at `DetailEngine.swift:471`. Measured on
`hazySky` at Dehaze −100 and −50 the two agree to **0.0000 EV everywhere**, because in a
genuinely hazy sky `1 − t ≥ skyness` already. The term only bites on a **clear** sky,
where `1 − t → 0` and `skyness → 1`: there the reference lays down `0.9·|amount|` of
airlight and the GPU lays down almost none — i.e. negative Dehaze adds atmosphere to
everything except the sky, on exactly the frame someone reaches for it. `hazySky` cannot
show this; it is the wrong proof frame for the negative branch and no other frame in the
fixed set contains a clear sky.

## 2. Findings

### D2-01 — shipping Texture selects by CONTRAST, not by SCALE, so the resolution-tracked band is inert and the sign of its divergence flips
severity: S2
confidence: measured
class: defect
size: M

**evidence**

The reference Texture is a raised-cosine window over à-trous scale index whose centre
moves with the frame — `DetailEngine.swift:1046-1052`,
`bandCenter = 1 + clamp(log2(longEdge/2560), -1, 2)`, weights at `:1067-1071`,
truncation-renormalised at `:271-284`. The gain per band is independent of the band's
amplitude.

The shipping path is `image − guidedSelfFilter(image, radius: max(round(longEdge·0.003), 2), ε)`
(`RenderGraph.swift:635-636`, `:659`, `:678-680`) fed to `Kernels.swift:339-352`. A **self-guided**
filter's transfer is `a = var/(var + ε)`, so the fraction of a signal that lands in the
"band" is `ε/(var + ε)` — a function of the signal's **contrast**, and only weakly of its
size. The radius barely participates.

Measured, Texture +100, `longEdge = 2560`, a pure sinusoid, gain = RMS contrast out / in:

| period 8 px, peak amplitude | reference | GPU | gpu/ref |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.05 EV | 1.685 | 1.797 | 1.066 |
| 0.10 EV | 1.684 | 1.597 | 0.948 |
| 0.20 EV | 1.680 | 1.298 | 0.773 |
| 0.40 EV | 1.541 | 1.080 | 0.701 |

**The reference is flat to 0.3% across a 4× contrast range; the GPU falls 28%.**

And the band shape, probed at 0.05 EV (below the coherence gate's 0.35 EV/px strength
floor, so this is band shape alone):

| period | ref @2560 | gpu @2560 | ref @6000 | gpu @6000 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 px | 1.308 | 1.717 | **1.000** | **1.718** |
| 4 px | 1.481 | 1.797 | 1.032 | 1.799 |
| 8 px | 1.685 | 1.797 | 1.244 | 1.797 |
| 16 px | 1.429 | 1.796 | 1.605 | 1.790 |
| 32 px | 1.155 | 1.527 | 1.508 | 1.787 |
| 64 px | 1.043 | 1.183 | 1.202 | 1.582 |

The GPU column is **the same numbers at both resolutions**. The reference's band centre
moves (level 1.00 → 2.23) and drops the 2-px scale to a weight of exactly 0 at 6000 px
(`bandWeight(0, 2.229, 1.6)`: `d = 1.393 ≥ 1 → 0`). The shipping path gives that same
2-px scale a gain of **1.718**.

At the amplitude the recorded TEXSPEC probe actually uses (20% modulation ≈ 0.263 EV
peak), swept at 2560 px, the ratio is the familiar under-delivery — 0.842 / 0.872 / 0.839
/ 0.708 / 0.700 / 0.755 / 0.824 / 0.935 / 0.995 at 2 / 3 / 4 / 6 / 8 / 12 / 16 / 24 / 32
px, the same shape docs/24 §gap-2 recorded at 96 px. At 0.05 EV it is 1.07–1.78
**over**-delivery. Both are the same defect read at two contrasts.

**how the owner sees it**: on a 24 MP file, Texture +100 boosts the 1–2 px noise scale by
72% — the scale the specified operator excludes entirely — while a fabric weave at 20%
contrast gets 1.30× where a low-contrast one at the same size gets 1.80×. So the slider is
strongest on flat skin, smooth sky and sensor noise and weakest on the actual texture it
is named for, and pushing it hard on a high-ISO frame reads as noise coming up rather than
bark coming forward. `BasicPanel.swift:539-541` promises "mid-sized detail — pores, bark,
weave — while leaving hard edges and soft bokeh alone"; the shipping operator has no
notion of "mid-sized" at all.

**fix**: this is the third band-port attempt, and commit `8324f37`'s conditions still
govern the order (make the gate tell a gradient from an edge; only then decouple
measurement radius from closure radius; re-apply `6cba229`'s window clamp; port
`Σ wℓ(sℓ − sℓ₊₁)` with realized-weight normalisation on BOTH paths together; re-derive
`testPresenceDoesNotRimAHardEdge`'s 0.30 EV bar). Files: `Kernels.swift` (a band-stack
gain kernel or five weighted `lumenDetailGainGated` passes), `RenderGraph.swift:625-716`,
`DetailEngine.swift:262-309` unchanged as the contract.

**the test that goes red today**: a contrast-invariance assertion, which nothing in the
suite has. In `TextureSpectrumProbeTests` (or a new `TexturePassbandTests`), sweep a
sinusoid of fixed period 8 px at peak amplitudes 0.05 / 0.10 / 0.20 EV — below the
coherence gate, so only the band is being read — through both paths at `longEdge` 2560
and assert `(max gain − 1)/(min gain − 1) < 1.15` per path. The reference passes at
**1.007** (1.685 / 1.684 / 1.680); the GPU fails at **2.68** (1.797 / 1.597 / 1.298).
Second assertion, same file: at
`longEdge = 6000`, period 2 px, amplitude 0.05 EV, assert the GPU gain is within 0.05 of
the reference's — reference 1.000, GPU 1.718 today.

### D2-02 — the three tests that own the presence contract measure it at a frame/`longEdge` pair neither renderer ever produces, which is why the band port has been reverted twice
severity: S2
confidence: measured
class: defect
size: S

**evidence**

1. `Tests/LumenPipelineTests/KernelGoldenTests.swift:518` and `:1079` both call
   `RenderGraph.applyPresence(input, detail: detail, longEdge: 1600)` on buffers of
   **128×64** and **64×64**. `longEdge` is what sizes every radius in the stage:
   `RenderGraph.swift:635` gives a fine base radius of `round(1600·0.003) = 5` px and

   window of `max(3/4, 1) = **1**` (`DetailEngine.swift:177`), and a band centre of
   `bandCenter(64) = 0` (`DetailEngine.swift:1049`, the clamp at −1 binding).

   Commit `8324f37`'s own message names this as the thing that killed the second port:
   *"the golden hands `applyPresence` a longEdge of 1600 with a 64 px frame, so the
   coherence window came out at 8 where the reference sizes it off the buffer and gets
   1."* Its measured table — measurement window 1 → rim 0.2668 EV, 2 → 0.1774, 4 →
   0.1148, 8 → 0.1104, discrimination lost at 6 — says the two windows are not
   interchangeable. The goldens have not moved since.

2. The floors themselves disagree. `DetailEngine.swift:177` floors the gate window at
   **1**; `RenderGraph.swift:1217` floors it at **2**. They diverge for every render

3. `TextureSpectrumProbeTests.swift:35-41` builds its probe at **96×32** with a single

   (`:129-133`) are `0.2 < gain < 5.0`. Both frame size and amplitude decide the answer
   (D2-01), so the probe's table is a reading at one point of a two-dimensional surface.
   docs/24 §gap-2 promoted one of those readings — "the feared finest-scale over-boost is
   REFUTED — the GPU under-delivers at 2 px (0.85)" — to a settled finding, and at 6000 px
   long edge and 0.05 EV contrast the same measurement reads **1.718 against 1.000**.

**how the owner sees it**: nothing on screen — this is why it matters. Two correct Texture
ports were reverted because the goldens said red, and the goldens were measuring a
configuration the app never renders. As long as this stands, the third attempt will cost
the same two cycles.

**fix**: (a) `KernelGoldenTests.swift:518, 1079` — pass `longEdge: source.width` (or
whatever `Options.longEdge` the same recipe would carry through `build`), not a literal
1600; (b) make `RenderGraph.structureRadius` floor at 1, matching
`DetailEngine.swift:177`, or move both floors onto one named constant on `DetailEngine`;
(c) give `TextureSpectrumProbeTests` an amplitude axis (0.05 / 0.10 / 0.20 / 0.40 EV) and
a resolution axis (2560 / 6000) and print the full grid.

**the test that goes red today**: a two-line unit test with no GPU needed —
`XCTAssertEqual(RenderGraph.structureRadius(longEdge: 128), max(max(Int(Double(128) * 0.02), 3) / 4, 1))`
fails (2 vs 1), and the same at 256 (2 vs 1). Then, on the macOS lane, re-run
`testPresenceDoesNotRimAHardEdge` with `longEdge: 128` instead of 1600: the trench it
measures moves from the 8-px-window value to the 1-px-window value, which by `8324f37`'s
table is 0.1104 → 0.2668 EV, straight through the 0.30 EV bar's headroom.

### D2-03 — Vignette ships 2 of the 5 controls docs/06 specifies, and Midpoint cannot be added as the gap table sizes it, because Midpoint and Feather are literally the same product in the geometry
severity: S2
confidence: measured
class: parity-gap
size: M (not S)

**evidence**

docs/06 §Vignette specifies five rows with LR's ranges and defaults: Amount (−3…+1 EV),
Midpoint (0–100, 50), Roundness (−100…+100, 0), Feather (0–100, 50), Highlight protection
(0–100, 50). `EffectsPanel.swift:131-174` draws **Amount and Feather**. Midpoint and
Highlight protection are named engine constants read by both paths
(`DetailEngine.swift:627`, `:631`; the kernel is handed
`DetailEngine.vignetteHighlightProtection` at `RenderGraph.swift:1318`). Roundness has no
representation anywhere — `grep -rn roundness Sources/` is empty for the vignette.

The gap table's D row says `no midpoint/roundness/highlights (by design: EffectsPanel.swift:9)`
and sizes the fix "midpoint + roundness · S". Both halves need correcting.

*By design* is an over-read. `EffectsPanel.swift:7-10` argues that the **style dropdown**
(Highlight Priority / Colour Priority) is unnecessary because pipeline position does that
job — which docs/06 also says, and which is right. It says nothing about the shaping
controls, and docs/06 keeps them explicitly: *"equal control vocabulary
(Midpoint/Roundness/Feather kept with LR's ranges and defaults)"*.

*Size S* is wrong, and this is the useful part. `DetailEngine.vignetteInnerRadius(feather:)`
(`:645-648`) is `clamp(1.5 · vignetteMidpoint · (1 − feather/100), 0, 0.98)` — Midpoint
and Feather enter as a **product**, and the outer edge is pinned at r = 1 by construction
(stated at `:642-644`: "which is what lets the GPU kernel keep its single `feather = 1 − inner`
scalar unchanged"). With one end of the ramp pinned there is exactly **one** free
parameter, so a Midpoint slider added on this geometry would be a second control on the
one axis Feather already drives. Measured over a 3:2 frame, frame-mean falloff:

| midpoint (feather fixed 50) | inner | mean | | feather (midpoint fixed 50) | inner | mean |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0.0000 | 0.5565 | | 100 | 0.0000 | 0.5565 |
| 25 | 0.1875 | 0.4350 | | 75 | 0.1875 | 0.4350 |
| 50 | 0.3750 | 0.2916 | | 50 | 0.3750 | 0.2916 |
| 75 | 0.5625 | 0.1437 | | 25 | 0.5625 | 0.1437 |
| 100 | 0.7500 | 0.0407 | | 0 | 0.7500 | 0.0407 |

Identical columns. Shipping both would violate docs/00 D3 (never two parallel tools for
one intent) with two sliders that are provably the same slider.

**how the owner sees it**: an LR refugee reaches for Midpoint and Roundness and finds
neither, and the one shaping control that is there does double duty without saying so. A
rectangular burn — Roundness −100, the "letterbox" vignette — is not reachable at all.

**fix**: LR parity here requires **unpinning the outer edge**, which is a geometry change
and a new kernel scalar, not just a recipe field. LR's two knobs are the falloff's
*centre* (Midpoint) and its *width* (Feather), so the honest shape is
`falloff = smoothstep(inner, outer, r)` with `inner = centre − halfWidth` and
`outer = centre + halfWidth`, and the two mapping constants chosen so that
(midpoint 50, feather 50) reproduces today's `inner = 0.375, outer = 1.0` exactly —
i.e. centre 0.6875, halfWidth 0.3125 at the defaults. Roundness is a second, separable
change: an exponent on the radius norm, `r = (|u|^p + |v|^p)^(1/p)`, p = 2 at roundness 0
running toward a supremum norm at −100 (rectangular) and toward a circle at +100.
Files: `DetailEngine.swift:627-702` (the falloff, its inner radius and the r norm),
`Kernels.swift:874-888` (`lumenVignette` gains `outer` and `roundness`; it currently
carries `feather` alone because one scalar was enough),
`RenderGraph.swift:1288-1322`, `RecipeLook.swift` (two fields, tolerant decode exactly as
`vignetteFeather` already does), `EffectsPanel.swift:131-174` (two rows).
Every existing recipe must still render byte-identically at midpoint 50 / roundness 0 —
`VignetteFeatherTests.testAnOldSidecarDecodesToTheDefaultAndRendersIdentically` is the
pattern and already asserts at `accuracy: 0`.

**the test that goes red today**: the geometry half can be pinned before any UI exists.
`testTheFalloffHasTwoFreeEndsNotOne` — assert that some (midpoint, feather) pair produces
an outer radius below 1.0. Today `DetailEngine.vignetteFalloff(radius:inner:)`
(`:700-702`) hard-codes `Num.smoothstep(inner, 1.0, radius)`, so the outer edge is 1.0 for
every setting and the assertion cannot be satisfied. Second, and the one that names the
duplication: `testMidpointAndFeatherAreNotTheSameAxis` — for the four pairs above, assert
`inner(midpoint: 25, feather: 50) != inner(midpoint: 50, feather: 75)`. They are equal to
the last bit today (both 0.1875), which is exactly why a Midpoint slider must not be added
to the current geometry.

confidence: traced
class: defect

(`:1867-1875`, `recipe.look.vignette = -1.0`), uses a default `Recipe()` with no crop, so

**how the owner sees it**: not directly. The consequence is that the P5 proof for

## 3. Checked and found correct

Worth recording, because several of these are places the audit could reasonably have
expected a defect and there is not one.

- **The vignette's own arithmetic is exact.** Every number in `DetailEngine`'s
  `vignetteFalloff` doc comment (`:657-700`) reproduces on an independent implementation:
  frame mean 0.2916 at the default feather (I get 0.2916), 38.81% of the frame at ≤10% of
  Amount (38.86), 3.15% at ≥90% (3.15), outer tenth carrying 6.86% of the burn (6.88),
  2.80% at feather 100 (2.80). The alternative-shape table reproduces to three figures for
  all five candidates, including the non-obvious "outer tenth" column, which is
  `f(1) − f(0.9)` — `t²` 0.294 (I get 0.2944), `t^1.5` 0.230 (0.2301), `5t²−8t³+4t⁴` 0.222
  (0.2221), `(3t²−2t³)^0.7` 0.049 (0.0485). The argument that every live-slope shape is
  *below* the Hermite everywhere (difference `s·t²(1−t) ≥ 0`) is correct as written.
  `VignetteResponseTests` and `VignetteFeatherTests` are, between them, the strongest test
  pair I read in this area: monotone over 401 settings at accuracy 0, corner gain
  `2^ev` to 1%, byte-identical old-sidecar rendering, fingerprint stability, and the
  feather field traced to rendered pixels.
- **Vignette GPU/reference geometry agrees.** `smoothstep(1 − feather, 1, r)` with
  `feather = 1 − inner` (`Kernels.swift:881`, `RenderGraph.swift:1293`) is exactly the
  reference's `smoothstep(inner, 1, r)`; both normalise per axis then scale by
  `1/√2` so r = 1 is the corner; the Amount clamp is read from
  `DetailEngine.vignetteAmountRange` on both paths (`RenderGraph.swift:1259`) rather than
  transcribed; highlight protection and its threshold are the same named constants on both
  (`RenderGraph.swift:1301-1318`).
- **`EffectsPanel`'s Amount slider reads the engine's range** (`:133`,
  `range: DetailEngine.vignetteAmountRange`) rather than restating −3…+1. That is the
  right pattern and it is used.
- **Dehaze's colour stability is real on the shipping path.** The magenta-sky fix is not
  reference-only: `Kernels.swift:146-172` neutralises against the airlight, inverts on
  luminance and applies a scalar ratio, and `RenderGraph.swift:842-846` normalises the
  dark channel per channel by the airlight (not by its scalar mean). A scalar multiply
  cannot rotate a hue. `KernelGoldenTests.testDehazeDoesNotRotateHue` (`:46`) asserts it
  on the GPU. The `hueRotation: 1.36` in `records/detail.dehaze.json` comes from the
  negative branch, which blends toward the airlight on purpose.
- **Presence stage order matches the reference.** `RenderGraph.applyPresence:604-618`
  runs Texture → Clarity → Dehaze, the order `DetailEngine.apply:243-245` defines. The
  comment records that Dehaze used to run first; it does not now.
- **Dehaze radii and ε conversions match.** Dark-channel window off the **short** edge on
  both (`RenderGraph.swift:760-761` vs `DetailEngine.swift:186`, `:205`); guided
  refinement at the reference's radius with `ε = 0.02/range²` (`RenderGraph.swift:868`);
  airlight as the masked mean of the **source** where the dark channel is brightest, He's
  definition, frozen for the render on both paths.
- **The coherence kernel matches the reference.** `Kernels.swift:284-299` is the
  unsquared ratio times the strength gate, with the same 0.05/0.35 constants passed from
  `RenderGraph.coherenceStrengthLow/High` (`:1221-1222`). The gate's *radius* is the one
  thing that still diverges (D2-02).
- **`BasicPanel`'s Presence captions are honest.** `:542-557` states the rim measurements
  rather than the halo-free claim; the "neither can halo" caption docs/audit `DETAIL-04`
  convicted is gone.
- **Soft Proof is fully wired**, contrary to the class of defect this audit is hunting:
  `SoftProof` → `AppState.activeSoftProof` → `RenderRequest` → `RenderPlan.softProof` →
  `SoftProofTransform` (`Sources/LumenCore/Export/SoftProofTransform.swift`), and both
  intents genuinely differ (perceptual builds an OKLab gamut boundary at `:72-79`;
  relative-colorimetric clips), `simulatePaperWhite` is read at `:123-131`, and the gamut
  warning is computed per pixel rather than baked, for a stated and measured reason.
- **Lens Corrections is the honest form.** One control, `lens.profile`, whose reader
  (`AppleRawSource`) exists; disabled with an explanatory tooltip on rendered files
  (`EffectsPanel.swift:445, 453`); `removeCA` and the seven `Defringe` rows removed rather
  than left live over a footnote. (One stale word: `:68-69` still says "**Two** controls
  in this panel write fields whose only reader is `AppleRawSource`", and `isRenderedFile`
  now gates one. Not worth a numbered finding.)
