## Findings

### A1-01 — Contrast +100 burns the top 1.9 stops of the scene to one flat white, and the panel, the spec and the test all say it cannot
severity: S2
confidence: measured
class: defect
size: M
evidence: `ToneEngine.contrastMapped` (`ToneEngine.swift:363-373`) relaxes the slope back
to 1 over `contrastRelaxStartEV = 4` … `contrastRelaxEndEV = 12` (`:357-358`), but the
display transform saturates at the **white anchor, +5 EV** — `normalizedCurve` clamps its
argument (`DisplayTransform.swift:281`) so every `X ≥ 1` returns exactly 1. The relax
window finishes seven stops past the only place it needed to have finished. Measured at
Contrast +100, Pivot 0, Neutral preset, default anchors:

| scene EV | Contrast 0 | Contrast +100 |
|---|---|---|
| +3.0 | 234.51 | 254.83 |
| +3.5 | 243.73 | **255.00** |
| +4.0 | 250.14 | **255.00** |
| +4.5 | 253.85 | **255.00** |
| +5.0 | 255.00 | 255.00 |
| −4.0 | 9.41 | **1.01** |
| −5.0 | 3.85 | **0.51** |
| −6.0 | 1.45 | **0.50** |
| −8.0 | 0.51 | 0.50 |

Solving `contrastMapped(t) = +5` gives **t = +3.1250**: everything from +3.125 EV up
renders as one identical code value where at Contrast 0 it held separation to +5. That is
**1.875 stops of highlight** collapsed. At the other end `contrastMapped(t) = −9` gives
**t = −5.9627**, so **3.037 stops of shadow** collapse onto display black. At Contrast +50
the same figures are 1.154 and 1.279 stops.

The three places that say otherwise: the slider's own help — *"the ends of the scale stay
pinned, so it cannot clip a highlight"* (`BasicPanel.swift:450-453`); `docs/04:273`, which
promises *"soft saturation of the slope's effect beyond ±4 EV from the pivot so extremes
compress instead of exploding"*; and **`testContrastPinsTheEndsOfTheScale`**
(`EngineTests.swift:256-264`), whose docstring states *"a contrast push can never clip a
highlight it was not asked to touch"*. That test is a tautology: it probes only
`±LumenLog.maxEV`, i.e. **±12 EV**, which is exactly where `smoothstep(4, 12, |d|)` is 1 by
construction — the one pair of points on the axis where the identity is guaranteed
whatever the constants are. It would stay green if `contrastRelaxEndEV` were raised to 100.
No test probes at the anchors the picture is actually formed against.
how the owner sees it: he pushes Contrast to give a flat scene some snap and the sky goes
to a single flat white with the cloud edges gone, while the shadows under the trees go to
solid black — the two failures the row's tooltip tells him it cannot produce. Backing off
to +50 still costs him a stop of sky. He reads it as "Contrast blows highlights here", which
is precisely what every other raw editor's Contrast avoids.
fix: `ToneEngine.swift` — denominate the relax window on the ANCHORS rather than on two
constants, so the slope is back to 1 by `whiteAnchorEV` and `blackAnchorEV` (e.g.
`relax = smoothstep(0.35·anchor, 0.95·anchor, |d|)` per side, solved so the derivative
stays positive — the widening at `:351-356` was done to keep monotonicity and an
anchor-relative window has to be re-solved against the same predicate). If the clipping is
kept as a decision, the tooltip and `docs/04:273` must be rewritten to say so. Test that
goes red with today's code: `EngineTests.testContrastDoesNotClipAtTheDisplayAnchors` —
`ToneEngine(tone: Tone(contrast: 100)).contrastMapped(whiteAnchorEV)` ≤ `whiteAnchorEV` and
`contrastMapped(blackAnchorEV)` ≥ `blackAnchorEV`; today it returns **+7.871 EV** against a +5 EV anchor and
**−10.709 EV** against a −9 EV one. Replace `testContrastPinsTheEndsOfTheScale`'s ±12 EV probes with the two
anchors in the same commit, or the tautology outlives the fix.

### A1-02 — The whole positive half of the Brights zone renders one identical picture, and the proof record already says so
severity: S2
confidence: measured
class: defect
size: M
evidence: `Zones.defaultPivots` puts Brights at scene **+4 EV** (`Recipe.swift:453-456`),
one stop under the white anchor, where the display transform has **4.86 code values** of
headroom left. Measured on the neutral ramp at the Neutral preset:

| scene EV | Brights 0 | +1 EV | +2 EV | +3 EV | −1 EV | −3 EV |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| +3.5 | 243.73 | 253.04 | **255.00** | **255.00** | 226.27 | 165.84 |
| +4.0 (its own pivot) | 250.14 | **255.00** | **255.00** | **255.00** | 234.51 | 168.58 |
| +4.5 | 253.85 | **255.00** | **255.00** | **255.00** | 243.73 | 189.51 |
| +5.0 | 255.00 | 255.00 | 255.00 | 255.00 | 250.14 | 207.45 |

From its own pivot upward, +1, +2 and +3 EV are byte-identical. The committed record says
the same thing in one number: `Proof/records/zones.bright.ev.json` carries
`"frontLoading": 0.9976` — **99.76 %** of the control's cumulative authority is delivered in
the negative half of its travel, so the whole of 0…+3 EV is worth **0.10 of 43.23 code
values**. `frontLoading` is *pinned* but never *judged* — `ProofRecord.swift:99` compares it
against the committed value, so 0.9976 is preserved as a fact rather than read as a
defect, and there is no floor on it anywhere (`ProofRunner.swift:142` writes it;
`ControlProofTests` asserts `authority`, `deadSteps`, `givenBack` and
`declaredPlateauSteps`). `deadSteps` is 0 only because the peak is taken over the whole
frame, where the ramp's midtones still creep. The registry's prose actively denies the finding —
`ProofRegistry.swift:838-844`: *"Light (+2 EV) and Bright (+4 EV) now live in display
compression and measure lower than the old placements did — that is the transform being
honest about highlights, **not the controls going dead**"*. Half of Bright is dead; the
number that proves it was already in the file.
how the owner sees it: he drags Brights up on a beach frame to open the sand and nothing
happens — not at +1, not at +3 — and drags it down and gets 80 code values immediately. A
bipolar slider that only works one way reads as broken, and there is nothing on the panel
or in the tooltip (`ZonesPanel.swift:110-113`) that says the top half is spent.
fix: two candidates, and the choice is a taste call the owner should make.
(a) Denominate the five zone pivots so the top zone sits where the display can still show a
lift — Brights at ~+2.5 EV instead of +4 — which is a `pipelineVersion` migration since
`Zones.defaultPivots` is a golden constant. (b) Keep the placement and make the positive
half asymmetric, as `ToneEngine.blackToneEV = 2.2` vs `whiteToneEV = 1.3` already does for
Whites/Blacks (`:94-98`), so +3 EV of Brights buys the same *visible* move as −3 EV does.
Test that goes red today: `ProofFrameTests` / `ControlProofTests` —
`testEveryZoneMovesInBothDirections`, asserting for each zone that
`|render(neutral) − render(high)|` is at least 25 % of `|render(low) − render(high)|`;
Brights measures **0.2 %**. A cheaper first version, no GPU needed:
`AccuracyProbeTests.testTheBrightZoneLiftsAtItsOwnPivot` — three settings at scene +4 EV
must produce three distinct display values.

### A1-03 — Moving Whites or Blacks silently drags every Zones pivot up to 1.5 stops, and the strip does not move
severity: S2
confidence: measured
class: defect
size: M
evidence: the zone pivots are stored on the **normalized** axis (`Recipe.swift:436-437`),
and `ToneEngine.normalizedAxis` maps that axis onto scene EV through the *current* anchors
— `(t − blackAnchorEV) / (whiteAnchorEV − blackAnchorEV)` (`ToneEngine.swift:377-381`) —
while Whites and Blacks move those anchors by ±1.5 EV each
(`ToneEngine.swift:121-124`, `whiteBlackRangeEV = 1.5`). `Zones.defaultPivots` is derived
once, from the DEFAULT anchors (`Recipe.swift:453-456`). Measured scene EV of each zone:

| Whites | Blacks | anchors | Darks | Shadows | **Midtones** | Lights | Brights |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0 | +5.00 / −9.00 | −4.00 | −2.00 | **0.00** | +2.00 | +4.00 |
| +100 | 0 | +3.50 / −9.00 | −4.54 | −2.75 | **−0.96** | +0.82 | +2.61 |
| 0 | −100 | +5.00 / −7.50 | −3.04 | −1.25 | **+0.54** | +2.32 | +4.11 |
| +100 | +100 | +3.50 / −10.50 | −5.50 | −3.50 | **−1.50** | +0.50 | +2.50 |
| −100 | −100 | +6.50 / −7.50 | −2.50 | −0.50 | **+1.50** | +3.50 | +5.50 |

Whites +100 alone moves the zone labelled **Midtones** from mid-grey to **−0.96 EV**, and
Brights from +4.00 to +2.61. `ZonePivotStrip` draws the crossfade in normalized x
(`ZonesPanel.swift:242-263`), so the handles stay exactly where they were while the tone
each one names has walked a stop — the panel's own claim that *"the picture and the maths
cannot disagree"* (`:214-216`) holds in x and fails in EV, which is the coordinate the
photographer edits in. `AccuracyProbeTests.testTheDefaultZonePivotsSitAtTheirDocumentedEVs`
(`AccuracyProbeTests.swift:116-127`) reads the pivots back through the axis but builds
`ToneEngine(tone: Tone(), zones: Zones())` — default anchors only — so it cannot see this.
how the owner sees it: he builds a zone set on a portrait, gets the face exactly where he
wants it, then nudges Whites to hold a specular — and the face moves. Going back to the
Zones panel shows every handle where he left it, so there is nothing to undo and no way to
attribute the change. Copying that zone set onto a frame whose Whites differ lands it on
different tones again.
fix: `ToneEngine.normalizedAxis` — denominate the zone axis on the DEFAULT anchors
(`defaultBlackAnchorEV` … `defaultWhiteAnchorEV`) rather than on the live ones, so a zone
pivot means a fixed scene EV and Whites/Blacks stop being a hidden zone-pivot control. The
grading wheels share this axis by design (`:377-378`), so the change has to be made for
both or the two panels stop agreeing; `GradingWheels.pivots` should be checked in the same
commit. Test that goes red today: `AccuracyProbeTests.testZonePivotsStayAtTheirEVWhenTheEndpointsMove`
— sweep Whites and Blacks over ±100 and assert every default pivot's scene EV stays within
0.05 EV of −4/−2/0/+2/+4; Midtones moves **0.96 EV** at Whites +100 and **1.50 EV** at
Whites +100 / Blacks +100.

### A1-04 — On rendered files the Linear preset throws the anchors away, so Whites and Blacks stop being white and black points and Highlights reaches half its shelf
severity: S3
confidence: measured
class: defect
size: S
evidence: every JPEG/HEIC/PNG/TIFF now opens on the Linear preset
(`AppState.swift:2941-2949`, `PhotoFormats.isRendered` at `:62-64`) — the K-091 fix, and it
is the right one. But `DisplayTransform.tone` short-circuits Linear before any anchor is
consulted: `if params.linear { return Num.clamp(x * white, 0, white) }`
(`DisplayTransform.swift:296-299`), and `apply` does the same at `:315-317`. `applyAnchors`
(`ToneEngine.swift:477-480`) still writes `whiteAnchorEV`/`blackAnchorEV` into the params
and `DisplayTransform.init` still solves `minEV`/`range`/`pivotX` from them (`:170-174`) —
all of it discarded. So on a rendered file the clipping point is pinned at linear 1.0 and
the Whites slider cannot move it, while its help says *"Sets the white clipping point —
where scene brights start rendering as pure white"* (`BasicPanel.swift:509-512`); Blacks'
says the same for the shadow end (`:517-519`). What survives is only their 1.3 / 2.2-stop
tonal shelves, which is the half `ToneEngine.swift:317-324` calls the addition, not the
mechanism.

The second half is the shelf denomination. A rendered file's brightest pixel is linear 1.0,
i.e. **t = +2.474 EV**, and the two upper shelves are cut against a +5 EV white anchor
(`ToneEngine.swift:311-315, 325-329`). Measured at the top of a JPEG's range:
`highlightWeight = 0.4922`, `whiteWeight = 0.4870`. Highlights −100 therefore applies
**−0.98 EV** at display white instead of the −2.00 EV the constant promises, and its shelf
reads as a broad ramp across the whole upper half of the picture rather than as highlight
recovery. The shadow end is unaffected (both lower shelves have saturated by −9.2 EV,
which is sRGB code 1).
how the owner sees it: he drops a phone HEIC in to fix a blown window. Whites does not move
where the white clips, Blacks does not move where the black clips, and Highlights at −100
pulls the whole upper half of the frame down by half as much as it does on a raw. The
tooltips promise all three.
fix: `DisplayTransform` — give the Linear branch the anchors it already has: map
`[blackAnchorEV, whiteAnchorEV]` onto `[black, white]` linearly in scene-linear terms
instead of `clamp(x·white)`, so "Whites owns the white point" survives the escape hatch.
Separately, `ToneEngine` should take the *source's* usable top (+2.474 EV for a rendered
file) as `defaultWhiteAnchorEV` when the source is rendered, so the two upper shelves span
the range the picture actually has — the same argument `RenderedImageSource` already makes
for ISO (`ImageSource.swift:122-131`). Test that goes red today:
`EngineTests.testWhitesMovesTheClipPointOnTheLinearPreset` — with `linear = true`,
`DisplayTransform(p).tone(0.18 · 2^3.5)` must differ between Whites 0 and Whites +100;
today both return exactly `white`.

### A1-05 — Whites' shelf sits inside Highlights' ramp, not above it, and Blacks' steepest point is 2.4 stops from where the file says it is
severity: S3
confidence: measured
class: defect
size: S
evidence: `ToneEngine.swift:79-83` states the design as a fact: *"Where the Whites and
Blacks shelves run… **They start above where Highlights and Shadows have already
saturated**, so the two controls act on different parts of the range instead of fighting
for the same one."* The constants say otherwise. Whites' shelf runs
`smoothstep(0.2·hi, 0.8·hi, t)` = **+1.0 … +4.0 EV** (`:82-83, 325-329`); Highlights'
shelf runs `smoothstep(0, 1.0·hi, t)` and saturates only at **+5.0 EV** (`:66, 311-315`).
Measured weights:

| t | highlightWeight | whiteWeight |
|---|---|---|
| +1.0 EV (Whites' shelf begins) | 0.1040 | 0.0000 |
| +2.0 | 0.3520 | 0.2593 |
| +3.0 | 0.6480 | 0.7407 |
| +4.0 EV (Whites saturates) | **0.8960** | 1.0000 |
| +5.0 | 1.0000 | 1.0000 |

Whites' entire travel lies inside Highlights' rising ramp; at Whites' own end point
Highlights is at 0.896, not at 1.0, and its slope there is still the largest it gets. The
same paragraph's companion claim for the lower pair carries a stale number:
`:86-89` says *"Shadows' steepest point is around −2.2 EV, Blacks' around −5.9"*. Shadows'
shelf is `smoothstep(0, 4.5, −t)`, steepest at **−2.25 EV** ✓; Blacks' is
`smoothstep(1.35, 5.58, −t)` (`blackShelfStart 0.15`, `blackShelfEnd 0.62`, |anchor| 9),
steepest at **−3.47 EV**, not −5.9. The two slopes peak **1.22 stops** apart, not the 3.7
the comment claims, so Shadows and Blacks contend over the same tones roughly three times
more than the file says.
how the owner sees it: Highlights −60 then Whites +60 does not give him back the brights he
just pulled down — the two sliders are working the same stretch of the range and cancelling,
where the panel's own tooltips ("Darkens or lifts the bright zone alone", "Sets the white
clipping point") lead him to expect two different jobs. He ends up walking both sliders in
turn to find a combination, which is exactly the Lightroom behaviour docs/04 says Lumen's
explicit windows exist to replace.
fix: either move `endShelfStart` up so Whites genuinely begins where Highlights has
saturated (which costs Whites authority — it measured 47.6 today, `tone.whites.json` — and
so needs `whiteToneEV` re-solved against the monotonicity predicate at `:197-248`), or
correct the three sentences at `:79-89` to describe the overlap that ships and re-derive
Blacks' stated steepest point from `blackShelfStart/End`. The second is the honest cheap
one and should land regardless; a comment that states a false geometric fact about the
control next to it is how `highlightShelfEnd` got its own paragraph of apology at `:12-18`.
Test that pins whichever is chosen: `EngineTests.testTheEndShelvesStartWhereTheZonalOnesFinish`
— assert `highlightWeight(endShelfStart·whiteAnchorEV) ≥ 0.99`; today it is **0.104**.
