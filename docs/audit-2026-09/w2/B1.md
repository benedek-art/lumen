## Checked and clean (stated because the brief asked)

- **CAT16 matches the published transform exactly.** `ColorSpaces.swift:147-150` is
  Li et al. 2017's M_CAT16 to all six decimals; `bradford` at `:154-157` is the
  published Bradford matrix; `adapt` at `:160-165` is the correct von-Kries form
  (`cone⁻¹ · diag(d/s) · cone`), and `matrix(to:)` only inserts it when the white
  points differ. No transcription error, no transposition, no missing normalisation.
- **The band seam does not ridge.** Weights are normalised at
  `ColorEngine.swift:540`, so Yellow+Green both at Sat +100 give chroma gain exactly
  **2.000 at 119.2°, 130°, 141.7°, 150° and 164.2°** — flat across the seam, identical
  to either band alone at its own centre. Hue +100 on both is **45.000° everywhere**
  across the seam. There is no ridge and no double-count at the default geometry.
- **Hue is exactly held by Mixer Hue/Sat/Lum, by Point Colour Sat/Lum, and by the
  additive halves of Vibrance and Saturation.** All five write OKLCh with `h` carried
  through untouched (`:989-993`, `:1092`, `Perceptual.swift:226-231`). The two controls
  that *do* rotate hue are B1-05 and B1-02 below.
- **Planckian branch (latent only).** `ColorSpaces.swift:299-301` uses Kim et al.'s
  2222–4000 K cubic for *all* t > 2222; the published >4000 K cubic is absent. Inside
  the range `locus()` actually evaluates it (≤4500 K) the error is **≤0.20 tint units
  at 4500 K, 0.04 at 4100 K** — below one slider step, so not a defect today. It is
  **2.33 tint units at 10000 K** for any future caller of `planckianLocus` directly.

## Findings

### B1-01 — Point Colour Range is not a selection: at Range 100 a swatch moves neutral grey at full strength
severity: S1
confidence: measured
class: defect
size: M
evidence: `ColorEngine.swift:1019-1025` (`sigmaL = 0.60·r`, `sigmaC = 0.25·r`, `sigmaH = 0.30·r`)
and `:1051-1064` (`d = ‖(dL/σL, dC/σC, dH/σH)‖`, `weight = 1 − smoothstep(0.5, 1, d)`).
Swatch sampled on a blue sky (OKLCh L 0.60, C 0.12, h 255°), Range = 100 — measured
selection weights:

| pixel | d | weight |
|---|---|---|
| the sampled colour | 0.000 | **1.000** |
| pure neutral grey, same L, C = 0 | 0.480 | **1.000** |
| near-neutral warm grey C = 0.02, h 60° | 0.515 | **0.997** |
| a colour 90° away in hue | 0.566 | **0.953** |
| skin L 0.70 C 0.08 h 56° | 0.684 | 0.692 |
| foliage L 0.45 C 0.12 h 140° | 0.720 | 0.591 |
| the exact opposite hue (h 75°), same L and C | 0.800 | **0.352** |

The hue term is chordal (`dH = 2√(C·C_t)·sin(Δh/2)`, `:1049`), so at ordinary photographic
chroma a full 180° hue error contributes only `0.2/0.30 = 0.67` of distance while the
plateau runs to 0.5 and the falloff to 1.0. Hue therefore cannot exclude anything on its
own. The other end of the travel is dead for the opposite reason: at **Range 0** the
sigma floor `1e-4` (`:1022-1024`) means only a bit-exact match moves, so the slider's
declared 0–100 travel is inert at the bottom and non-selective at the top.
how the owner sees it: he clicks a blue sky, drags Luminance to −100 to deepen it, and
the grey concrete, the white wall and the skin in the same frame all darken with it —
the grey by **128 → 58 sRGB code values, a 70-level move at full weight**. The control
reads as broken selection, not as a global slider, so he has no way to attribute it.
fix: `ColorEngine.compiledSwatches` — scale the axes so the master Range spans a
*photographic* tolerance, not the whole space (σ_L ≈ 0.18, σ_C ≈ 0.10 at Range 100), and
make the hue term angular rather than chordal above the chroma gate so hue can exclude.
Test that goes red with the old sigmas restored: `ColorScienceTests` —
`testPointColourSelectsOnlyItsOwnColour`, asserting weight ≥ 0.9 on the sampled colour,
**≤ 0.05 on a pure neutral of the same lightness and ≤ 0.05 at 90° of hue**, at Range 100.
No test today touches `range`, `variance` or swatch selectivity at all (`ColorScienceTests`
has no Point Colour case; `RobustnessTests:345` only checks aliveness).

### B1-02 — Point Colour's shifts bypass the chroma gate the engine's own header says every hue-selective tool shares
severity: S1
confidence: measured
class: defect
size: S
evidence: `ColorEngine.swift:1071` computes `let gate = Self.chromaGate(lch.C)` **inside
`if s.q != 0`** — the Variance branch only. The three shift lines below it are ungated:
`:1088 h += weight * s.shiftH`, `:1089 C = max(0, C * (1 + weight * s.shiftS / 100))`,
`:1091 L += weight * (s.shiftL/100) * lumKappa * lumShape(L)`. The file header at
`:8-10` states the opposite as a design law: *"they share … the chroma gate, so
near-neutral pixels — whose hue is numerically noise — are never rotated by a
hue-selective tool"*. `applyMixer:923-927` does apply it; Point Colour does not.
Measured on a warm grey at C = 0.02 (exactly `gateLoChroma`, where the Mixer's authority
is **0.000**): selection weight 0.997, hue shift +60° applied as **+59.8°**, sRGB
`(138,126,117) → (126,130,117)` — a **12-code-value** swing from warm to green on a
pixel the Mixer refuses to touch.
how the owner sees it: a Point Colour swatch placed on a shirt tints his neutral wall,
his grey card and his shadow noise. Because the shift is largest where chroma is
lowest-signal, it lands hardest on smooth flat areas, where blotching is most visible.
fix: hoist `let gate = Self.chromaGate(lch.C)` out of the `q != 0` branch in
`applySwatch` and fold it into `weight` for the hue and saturation shifts (leave the
lightness shift ungated only if that is a stated decision). Test:
`testPointColourDoesNotRotateNearNeutrals` — a C = 0.02 pixel under a ±60° hue shift must
move < 1 sRGB code value; it goes red the moment the gate is removed again.

### B1-03 — Negative Saturation silently switches the whole B&W mix off
severity: S1
confidence: measured
class: defect
size: M
evidence: `apply` runs `applyVibranceSaturation` **before** `applyBlackAndWhite`
(`ColorEngine.swift:385-386`), and the mix's per-band gain is multiplied by the chroma
gate of the *incoming* pixel: `:1245-1247` `let gate = Self.chromaGate(lch.C)` …
`gain += w[i] * gate * (blackAndWhiteBands[i]/100)`. Saturation is a chroma scale, so it
drives that gate to zero. Blue band at −80 on a sky at OKLCh C = 0.12:

| Saturation | chroma reaching S9's B&W step | gate | grey rendered |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0.120 | 1.000 | 128 → **59** (69 code values) |
| −50 | 0.060 | 1.000 | 128 → 59 |
| −75 | 0.030 | 0.156 | 128 → 120 (**8**) |
| −80 | 0.024 | 0.028 | 128 → 127 (**1**) |
| −100 | 0.000 | 0.000 | 128 → 128 (**0**) |

All eight band sliders are exactly inert at Saturation −100 and already 88 % dead at −80.
how the owner sees it: "desaturate, then turn on B&W and mix the sky down" is the
standard route into a black-and-white edit. He gets a flat grey conversion, eight
sliders that move nothing, and no message. The same happens to any Look pasted onto a
frame whose Saturation is down.
fix: `applyBlackAndWhite` must read the band weights and the gate off the **stage
input** (the value `apply` received) rather than off the post-saturation pixel — pass
`c` through, as the variance kernel already threads `localMean`. Test:
`testTheBlackAndWhiteMixSurvivesNegativeSaturation` — Blue −80 must move the rendered
grey by the same ±2 code values at Saturation 0 and at −100. There is no B&W behavioural
test at all today (`grep -n "func test" ColorScienceTests.swift` returns no B&W case;
`docs/24 §8` lists the band-selectivity golden as gap #1).

### B1-04 — One band's ring handle silently steals its neighbours' authority
severity: S2
confidence: measured
class: defect
size: M
evidence: `bandWeights` normalises by the raw sum (`ColorEngine.swift:540`), so widening
band *i* raises the denominator at hues that belong to band *i±1*. Dragging **Red**'s
core from the default 22.5° to the maximum 44° (`bandCoreMaxDegrees:127`), everything
else untouched:

| measured at | weight before | weight after | authority lost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Orange's own hue centre (74.23°) | 1.0000 | **0.5027** | **49.7 %** |
| Magenta's own hue centre (344.23°) | 1.0000 | **0.5027** | **49.7 %** |
| Red's own hue centre | 1.0000 | 1.0000 | 0 |

Concretely: Orange Saturation +100 drops from chroma gain 2.000 to **1.503**, Orange
Hue +100 from **45.0° to 22.6°**. Widening only Red's *feather* (15° → the 60° maximum,
`:129`) costs the same two neighbours **40.9 %** each. And if all eight cores are widened
together every band loses at its own centre — measured 1.0000 → **0.3358** at core 44°,
with the loss beginning above core 30° (core+feather > 45°).
how the owner sees it: he widens Red to catch a brick wall, then goes to Orange and
finds the slider does half of what it did five minutes ago on the same photo. Nothing on
the ring says so — the ring draws each band's own arc, not the normalised weight.
fix: either (a) draw the *normalised* weight on the ring so the theft is visible (the
data is already there — `bandWeights(hue:arcs:)` is public precisely so the UI can), or
(b) renormalise per band so a band's weight at its own centre is pinned at 1 and only
the seam redistributes. Test: `testWideningOneBandDoesNotWeakenItsNeighbours` — assert
`bandWeights(hue: centre[i], arcs:)[i] ≥ 0.99` for every *i* under every legal arc; it
goes red against today's code at core > 30°.

### B1-05 — Density makes every positive Saturation move rotate hue, and it is on by default
severity: S2
confidence: measured
class: defect
size: S
evidence: `applyVibranceSaturation:1186-1190` blends the hue-preserving UCS push against
`subtractivePush` (`:1131-1144`), a per-channel signed gamma on `c/maxComponent` that is
defined in RGB and preserves neither hue nor lightness. `density` defaults to **50**
(`Recipe.swift:298`), so the blend is live on an untouched recipe. Measured at the
shipped defaults, Saturation +50:

| patch | hue rotation | ΔL | max sRGB code delta vs density 0 |
|---|---|---|---|
| skin (L 0.70 C 0.08 h 56°) | **−1.42°** | −0.0127 | 5 |
| blue sky | −0.17° | −0.0330 | 15 |
| red (C 0.20) | **−2.56°** | −0.0020 | 9 |
| foliage | +1.39° | −0.0055 | 2 |

At Saturation +100 / Density 100 the same skin patch rotates **−6.23°** and moves **27
code values**; the blue sky moves **54**. The darkening is the documented point of the
model (`ColorEngine.swift:21-24`); the *hue rotation* is nowhere declared — the header's
invariant #2 and `docs/24 §3` both describe Saturation as holding hue, and `docs/20 §P4`
makes "no hue rotation where it claims to move chroma only" a proof condition.
how the owner sees it: he saturates and his skin tones walk toward red while his foliage
walks toward cyan, a few degrees at a time, on a slider whose panel says nothing about
it. On a face this is the visible half of "why do my skin tones go orange when I push
colour".
fix: either declare it (panel note + `docs/24 §4`) or make the subtractive branch
hue-anchored — take the UCS `h` of the additive result and re-impose it on the blended
RGB. Test: `testDensityDoesNotRotateHueBeyondItsDeclaredBound` sweeping density 0…100 at
Saturation +100 on the eight band-centre hues of `tonalColourWedge`, asserting
|Δh| ≤ the declared bound; it goes red at 6.23° with today's code.

### B1-06 — "Lumen UCS" runs Nayatani's H-K term at 1/19 to 1/55 of its published magnitude
severity: S2
confidence: measured
class: defect
size: M
evidence: `Perceptual.swift:185-190` feeds Nayatani's VAC form
`Γ = 1 + (−0.1340·q(θ) + 0.0872·K_Br)·S` with `S = chroma·4.0` and then multiplies the
whole bracket by `·0.1`, i.e. `S_effective = 0.4·C_OKLab`. Nayatani's `S` is the CIE 1960
saturation `S_uv = 13·√((u−u_n)² + (v−v_n)²)`. Measured on the same colours, through this
engine's own Rec.2020 → xy → uv chain:

| colour | true `S_uv` | Lumen's `s` | ratio | published Γ | Lumen's Γ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| blue sky, C 0.12 | 0.902 | 0.0480 | **18.8×** | 1.090 | **1.0048** |
| red, C 0.20 | 4.384 | 0.0800 | **54.8×** | 1.439 | **1.0080** |
| sRGB blue primary, C 0.313 | 2.714 | 0.1252 | **21.7×** | 1.272 | **1.0125** |

Across all hues at C = 0.40 — "about as saturated as real surface colour gets" per the
code's own comment — the factor spans only **1.0111…1.0194**, and the *hue-dependent*
part, which is the entire reason the term exists ("saturated yellows read brighter than
equally-luminant blues", `:168`), spans **0.84 %**. `K_Br` is a constant, so ~70 % of even
that 1.9 % is a hue-flat gain that cancels out of `fromRGB`/`toRGB` round trips.
how the owner sees it: he does not. That is the finding. Every claim built on Lumen UCS
— docs/05 §"saturation moves hold *perceived* brightness constant", the header's
"the reason saturated blues here do not read as if they dimmed" (`ColorEngine.swift:19-20`)
— is being delivered at about one percent of the effect the cited model predicts.
Vibrance and Saturation are, to within 1 %, plain OKLab chroma scales.
fix: derive `S` from the pixel's actual `S_uv` (the working-space → uv chain already
exists in `Chromaticity.uv`) or state the `4.0 · 0.1` pair as a deliberate 40× damping
with the reason, in `Perceptual.swift` and `docs/24 §substrate`. Test:
`testHKBrightnessMatchesNayataniOnPublishedSamples` — three colours with hand-computed
`S_uv`, asserting Γ within 10 % of the VAC value; red goes red at 1.008 vs 1.439.
`docs/24:13` flags these constants as uncited but records no magnitude; this is the number.

### B1-07 — The B&W mix has no level normalisation, so it separates colours from greys instead of re-weighting them
severity: S2
confidence: measured
class: parity-gap
size: M
evidence: `applyBlackAndWhite:1247` accumulates `gain = 1 + Σ wᵢ·gate·(bandᵢ/100)` and
`:1253` returns `base · gain`. `Σwᵢ = 1`, so the gain is a pure *addition* on top of
unity and nothing renormalises it. Measured:

| patch | all bands 0 | all +50 | all +100 | all −100 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| blue sky, C 0.12 | 128 | 154 | **175** | **0** |
| neutral grey, C 0 | 128 | 128 | **128** | **128** |
| pale skin, C 0.05 | 157 | 184 | 207 | 65 |

Moving all eight sliders together — which is how a photographer sets overall contrast in
a channel mixer — brightens every chromatic pixel by up to **+0.45 EV** while every
neutral in the same frame stays exactly put, and at −100 drives colour to code 0 while
greys are untouched. The channel-mixer tradition the panel is modelled on normalises the
weights to preserve level (RawPedia Channel Mixer, cited in `docs/24 §8`).
how the owner sees it: his B&W conversion changes exposure as he mixes, and the sky
clips or blocks up against a wall that never moves. He compensates with Exposure, which
moves both, and can never get the relationship he wanted.
fix: normalise the gain against the frame's mean band composition, or subtract the mean
band value from each so a uniform move is a no-op (`gain = 1 + Σwᵢ·gate·(bandᵢ − b̄)/100`,
with a separate overall-brightness control if one is wanted). Test:
`testAUniformBWMixIsALevelNoOp` — all eight bands at +50 must leave every patch within
±2 code values; red today at 47.

### B1-08 — Band names sit up to 27° off the colours they name; foliage green is 61 % "Yellow"
severity: S2
confidence: measured
class: parity-gap
size: L
evidence: `bandAnchorDegrees = 29.23`, `bandSpacingDegrees = 45` (`ColorEngine.swift:101-102`)
put the eight centres at 29.2 / 74.2 / 119.2 / 164.2 / 209.2 / 254.2 / 299.2 / 344.2°.
Measured membership at the default geometry, converting each sRGB colour through the
engine's own working-space context:

| colour | OKLCh hue | dominant band | its weight | runner-up |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| sRGB green | 142.50° | Green | **0.502** | Yellow 0.498 (an exact 50/50 split) |
| foliage (0.15, 0.35, 0.08) | 135.57° | **Yellow** 0.610 | | Green 0.390 |
| sRGB cyan | 194.77° | Aqua 0.693 | | Green 0.307 |
| sRGB magenta | 328.36° | Magenta 0.629 | | Purple 0.371 |

Pure green lands within 1.4° of the Yellow/Green core boundary, and ordinary foliage sits
*inside Yellow*. LrC and DxO place their wedges on perceptual colour names
(`docs/02 §2.8`, `docs/03 §2.4`).
how the owner sees it: he drags "Green" to deepen leaves and gets 39 % of the move he
asked for, with the other 61 % waiting under a slider labelled Yellow. Everything he
knows from Lightroom about which slider to reach for is wrong by one band in the greens.
fix: re-anchor the centres on measured perceptual name centroids and bump
`pipelineVersion` (the constant is golden-locked at `:99-102` precisely so this is a
deliberate migration), or ship the eyedropper `docs/05` calls the Mixer's primary entry
so the photographer never has to guess. Test:
`testEachBandOwnsTheColourItIsNamedFor` — assert `dominantBand(for:)` returns Green for
sRGB green and for foliage, Yellow for sRGB yellow, and that the winner's weight ≥ 0.6;
it goes red on both green cases today. (Confirms `docs/audit/colour.md` COLOR-01 with
fresh measurements; that row carries no numbers.)
