# COLOR — Per-Slider Accuracy Dossier

Repo `/home/user/lumen` @ `4616424`. Scope: Temp & Tint, Vibrance, Saturation, Density, Protect Skin, HSL Mixer, Point Color, B&W Mixer, Color-Grade Wheels (+ luminance, blending, advanced grid), Printer Lights, Primaries. Verdicts: **CORRECT** (matches multi-source consensus), **DIVERGES** (differs — stated whether defensible design or defect), **UNPROVEN** (no test/fixture pins the claim). Attribution rule observed throughout: *documented* = stated by a cited external source; *inferred* = reverse-engineered, community consensus, or Lumen's own derivation. Note: `helpx.adobe.com` and `docs.darktable.org` are egress-blocked from this environment; where cited, their content was obtained through search-result excerpts, not full-page fetches, and is marked accordingly.

**Shared substrate (read first).** Hue-selective tools (Mixer, Point Color, B&W band weights) run in OKLCh; saturation-class tools (Vibrance/Saturation, grade Luminance, grid) run in "Lumen UCS" = OKLCh + Nayatani Helmholtz–Kohlrausch brightness `J = L·(1 + k(h)·C)` (`Sources/LumenCore/Color/Perceptual.swift:151-233`). OKLab matrices are Ottosson's published ones (documented: [Ottosson, "A perceptual color space for image processing"](https://bottosson.github.io/posts/oklab/); [Wikipedia, Oklab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oklab_color_space)) with two Lumen-specific corrections: chroma rows renormalized to exact zero sum (`Perceptual.swift:81-97`) and white-adaptation of the RGB→LMS chain (`Perceptual.swift:101-124`) — both ~1e-8/1e-4-scale, both *inferred* engineering, both defensible (they make "neutral has zero chroma" exact). The H-K predictor is Nayatani's VAC/VCC family (documented existence: [Nayatani 1997, *Simple estimation methods for the H-K effect*](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/(SICI)1520-6378(199712)22:6%3C385::AID-COL6%3E3.0.CO;2-R); [Nayatani 2006, VAC vs VCC clarification](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/col.20194)), but the coupling `s = C·4.0` and gain `·0.1` (`Perceptual.swift:172-177`) are **uncited calibration constants** — nothing pins them to published VAC data (audit COLOR-32, still true). Working space linear Rec.2020, luminance weights derived per SMPTE RP 177 construction (`Sources/LumenCore/Color/ColorSpaces.swift:69-91`).

## 1. Temp & Tint (White Balance)

**Expected math (industry consensus).**
- *CCT/tint geometry* — documented: CCT is defined by the normal to the Planckian locus in CIE 1960 UCS; Duv is signed distance from the locus, **positive = above = greenish, negative = below = pinkish/magenta** ([Ohno 2013, *Practical Use and Calculation of CCT and Duv*, LEUKOS 10(1)](https://www.nist.gov/publications/practical-use-and-calculation-cct-and-duv); [CORM 2011 formulae](https://cormusa.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/04/CORM_2011_Calculation_of_CCT_and_Duv_and_Practical_Conversion_Formulae.pdf)). Robertson (1968) computes CCT via isotherm tables in uv ([colour-science implementation of Robertson/DNG method](https://www.colour-science.org/api/0.3.3/html/_modules/colour/temperature/cct.html)).
- *Planckian locus fit* — documented: Kim et al. cubic approximation, x(T) in two branches (1667–4000 K, 4000–25000 K), y(x) in three branches ([Wikipedia, Planckian locus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planckian_locus)).
- *Daylight locus* — documented: CIE D-series `x_D` polynomials (4000–7000 K, 7000–25000 K) and `y_D = −3.000x² + 2.870x − 0.275` ([Wikipedia, Standard illuminant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_illuminant); [Patapom, Color Illuminants](https://patapom.com/blog/Colorimetry/Illuminants/)).
- *Adobe scale conventions* — documented: for raw files LR's Temp slider is denominated in Kelvin (2000–50000), Tint −150…+150, +Tint = magenta, −Tint = green ([Adobe LrC Help, Image tone and color](https://helpx.adobe.com/lightroom-classic/help/image-tone-color.html) — via search excerpt; [Adobe Community thread on the Kelvin scale](https://community.adobe.com/t5/lightroom-classic/kevlin-scale-to-100-100-scale-conversion/m-p/9249239)). The DNG SDK's `dng_temperature` maps xy↔(temp,tint) via Robertson's uv tables with tint scale constant `kTintScale = −3000` — i.e. **one tint unit = 1/3000 of a uv unit**, offset along the isotherm ([DNG SDK](https://helpx.adobe.com/camera-raw/digital-negative.html), discussed with code reference in [DPReview forum, "What is 'tint' in white balance?"](https://www.dpreview.com/forums/threads/what-is-tint-in-white-balance.4385471/)). Note the Kelvin *axis* is perceptually even in mireds (documented, same Ohno paper — Robertson's method iterates in reciprocal temperature).
- *Adaptation transform* — documented: CAT16 is the current-generation von Kries-style CAT (Li et al. 2017, [*Comprehensive color solutions: CAM16, CAT16, and CAM16-UCS*](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/col.22131)). Adobe's pipeline historically uses Bradford/linear-Bradford (documented in DNG spec); CAT16 > Bradford is *inferred* preference, defensible.

**What Lumen does.**
- Locus: Kim et al. x-fit verbatim (`ColorSpaces.swift:321-328`), Kim y-fits for the two low branches (`:293-303`); CIE daylight polynomials verbatim (`:309-319`). Below 3500 K pure Planck, above 4500 K pure daylight, smoothstep crossfade between (`:281-289`). Slider range 2000–50000 K, tint ±150 soft (`:265-268`).
- Tint: `1 unit = 0.05/150 = 3.333e-4` of CIE-1960 **v** (`:272`) — numerically identical to DNG's 1/3000 uv. Offset applied to v at constant u (`:430-436`), not along the Robertson isotherm; inverse recovers temperature by u-match, coarse-to-fine in mired space (`:451-486`).
- Sign convention: +tint moves the *target illuminant* above the locus (greener, +Duv), and since the engine adapts **from the target to the as-shot neutral** (`Sources/LumenCore/Engine/WhiteBalanceEngine.swift:41-52`), the rendered image shifts magenta — matching LR's UI direction. Corroborating: Lumen's WB presets are LR's canonical numbers (Fluorescent 3800/+21, Daylight 5500/+10, `Sources/LumenApp/BasicPanel.swift:46-51`), which only mean the same picture if the tint unit matches Adobe's.
- Adaptation: CAT16 cone matrix (`ColorSpaces.swift:147-150`), von Kries in cone space (`:160-165`), full matrix `space.fromXYZ · adapt · space.toXYZ` composed into the fused S6 linear stage with exposure and printer lights (`WhiteBalanceEngine.swift:191-208`, `Sources/LumenCore/Engine/RenderPlan.swift:120-129`). Decode always at camera-reference WB (`Sources/LumenPipeline/AppleRawSource.swift:150-151`); as-shot neutral read from `CIRAWFilter.neutralTemperature/.neutralTint` (`AppleRawSource.swift:80-81`).
- Magenta guard: `tintConeFloor = 0.15` (`ColorSpaces.swift:364`) caps positive tint via bisection on the unguarded CAT16 cone response (`tintLimit(kelvin:)`, `:376-399`; `clampedTint`, `:402-405`) so the S-cone never crosses zero (which flipped the blue gain negative and inverted the frame). Effective cap: +36 at 2000 K, +87 at 3200 K, +156 at 5500 K (in-code measurements, `:355-363`). The panel's tint range stays ±150/±300 with **no UI surfacing of the clamp** (`BasicPanel.swift:126-133`; deliberate, argued at `ColorSpaces.swift:422-429`).
- Display/first-drag contract: the row shows the file's real neutral, not a fabricated 5500 (`WhiteBalanceEngine.swift:109-116`); Temp slider is mired-spaced (`.reciprocal`, `BasicPanel.swift:102-119`); eyedropper solves by search against the forward model, returns tint clamped ±150 (`WhiteBalanceEngine.swift:127-183`).
- Tests: `Tests/LumenCoreTests/TintGuardTests.swift:36-165` (nine properties incl. no-inversion, green never clamped, eyedropper honesty), `ColorScienceTests.swift:159-206` (locus monotone, passes near standard illuminants, temperature round-trip), cross-platform WB fixture (`EngineMathFixtureTests.swift:237`), proof-registry aliveness for `raw.temp`/`raw.tint` (`Proof/ProofRegistry.swift:282,295`).

**Verdict.** Core math **CORRECT** against published references (Kim fit, CIE daylight polynomial, 1/3000-uv tint unit, Kelvin display, mired-spaced travel, CAT16 as published). Three **DIVERGES — defensible, documented design**: (a) Kelvin above ~4500 means *daylight-locus* Kelvin, where Adobe's DNG math is Planckian/Robertson throughout — same displayed number ≈ Duv +0.003 (≈10 tint units) apart from ACR in the daylight range; (b) tint offsets at constant u rather than along the Robertson isotherm — small angular difference, self-consistent with Lumen's own inverse; (c) CAT16 instead of Adobe's Bradford — a deliberate quality choice. One **DIVERGES — defect-class (known issue)**: the magenta half silently saturates at `tintLimit(kelvin:)` on warm frames — at 2000 K over three-quarters of the +tint travel is inert with no readout, no detent, no help text. **UNPROVEN**: the as-shot pathway. `CIRAWFilter.neutralTint`'s unit is undocumented by Apple and has never been checked against Lumen's 1/3000-uv unit on a real file — there are no RAW fixtures — so "Shot at X K, tint Y" and the first-drag no-jump contract rest on an unverified unit equivalence (the LR-preset match above is circumstantial, not proof).

**Gaps (ranked).** 1. Acquire RAW fixtures (one tungsten, one daylight, one fluorescent) and verify Apple's `neutralTemperature/neutralTint` against Lumen's forward model — a grey card shot must solve to ≈ the as-shot pair. 2. Surface the tint clamp in the UI (dim the inert slider region past `tintLimit`, or show "effective +36" readout). 3. Golden the Lumen-vs-ACR white point at 6500 K/0 and document the daylight-vs-Planck delta as intended. 4. Pin `tintUnitInV == 1/3000` with a comment citing the DNG SDK and a test, so a refactor can't silently rescale saved recipes. 5. Extend the Kim y-fit's third branch (4000–25000 K) or note that the 2222–4000 branch is extrapolated across the 4000–4500 K blend window.

## 2. Vibrance

**Expected math.** Documented Adobe semantics: Vibrance boosts saturation *non-uniformly* — most on muted colors, minimizing clipping as colors approach full saturation, with reduced effect on skin tones ([Adobe LrC Help, Image tone and color](https://helpx.adobe.com/lightroom-classic/help/image-tone-color.html) — via excerpt; [Digital Photography School, Vibrance vs Saturation](https://digital-photography-school.com/vibrance-vs-saturation-in-plain-english/); [SLR Lounge](https://www.slrlounge.com/vibrance-vs-saturation-what-is-the-difference/)). No vendor documents the exact weighting curve — any specific falloff is *inferred*.

**What Lumen does.** `applyVibranceSaturation` (`Sources/LumenCore/Engine/ColorEngine.swift:1061-1108`): amount = `vibrance/100 · lowChroma(C) · protection`, with `lowChroma = 1 − smoothstep(0.05, 0.25, C)` (`:1015-1017`, constants `:224-225`), skin protection at **both signs** (deliberate, argued `:1084-1089`), and positive pushes additionally tapered by the brightness rolloff (`:1090-1091`). The move itself is a chroma scale in Lumen UCS holding H-K brightness and hue (`shapedChromaScale`, `:1130-1139`), through the sat-vs-sat compression ratio (`satCompress`, `:1022-1027`).

**Verdict.** **CORRECT** in documented semantics (low-saturation weighting ✓, skin protection ✓, clipping resistance via the compression knee ✓); the specific constants (0.05/0.25 window, knee 0.18, ceiling 0.34) are Lumen's own, *inferred*, and there is no industry number to diverge from. Holding H-K brightness constant is a deliberate, internally-documented improvement over LR (whose internals are undocumented — comparison is qualitative only). **UNPROVEN at the property level**: no test asserts a muted color gains more than a saturated one — replace `lowChroma(...)` with `1.0` and the suite stays green (audit COLOR-30, still true at head; only aliveness via `ProofRegistry.swift:264` and the protect-skin attenuation test at `ColorScienceTests.swift:499` cover this slider).

**Gaps (ranked).** 1. Property test: at +50 Vibrance, ΔC(muted patch) > ΔC(saturated patch) > ≈0 for C≥0.25. 2. Property test: J (H-K brightness) invariant under ±Vibrance to tolerance. 3. Pin the H-K calibration constants (`C·4.0`, `·0.1`) against published Nayatani VAC data or document them as tuned. 4. Register `color.density`-style negative-side behavior of Vibrance in the proof registry evidence sheets.

## 3. Saturation

**Expected math.** Documented Adobe semantics: Saturation adjusts all colors *equally* (−100 = monochrome, +100 = doubled saturation) ([Adobe LrC Help](https://helpx.adobe.com/lightroom-classic/help/image-tone-color.html) — via excerpt; [The Lens Lounge, Vibrance vs saturation](https://thelenslounge.com/vibrance-vs-saturation/)). The −100 → grayscale endpoint is the hard contract; the "equal boost" on the push side is the LR convention, not a colorimetric standard.

**What Lumen does.** Push: `saturation/100 · lumSatRolloff(J) · protection`, pull: raw amount, unprotected and unrolled (`ColorEngine.swift:1092`). The chroma move is the ratio-form `shapedChromaScale` (`:1130-1139`): gain 0 ⇒ C = 0 exactly at every chroma (fixing the increment-form residue defect the header documents), gain 1 an exact fixed point, asymptotic resistance past the knee (`satCompress`, `:1022-1027`, knee 0.18 → ceiling 0.34). Brightness shaping: `lumSatRolloff` (`:1033-1041`) — shadow floor `smoothstep(0.02, 0.20)`, highlight taper from 0.86 falling to an asymptotic floor 0.35, never zero (constants `:242-249`); applies to *pushes only*, so −100 reaches true B&W everywhere including highlights and skin (tested: `ColorScienceTests.swift:469` on skin; protect-skin excluded from the pull — the COLOR-25 defect is fixed at head, `:1092` with rationale `:1077-1089`). Everything holds H-K perceived brightness (Lumen UCS).

**Verdict.** **DIVERGES — defensible, deliberate.** The −100 contract (the only hard documented convention) **holds and is tested**. The push side is intentionally *not* LR's uniform scale: already-saturated colors resist, highlights and deep shadows saturate less, and perceived brightness is held — all documented in-code as the design ("expensive colour"), none of it contradicting a published standard, all of it a visible behavioral difference from LR that should be stated in release notes rather than discovered. Constants are *inferred/tuned*. Fixture coverage exists (`EngineMathFixtureTests.swift:53,177,209` — rolloff, shaped scale, and public-path saturation against cross-platform fixtures) plus proof aliveness (`ProofRegistry.swift:257`).

**Gaps (ranked).** 1. Property test that +Saturation never *decreases* any pixel's chroma and never changes hue (hue-stability is the headline claim vs LR's HSV-ish shifts). 2. A side-by-side documented note (user-facing) that Lumen's push is compressed, not linear — "Lightroom or better" requires the difference be intentional-and-stated. 3. Golden the knee/ceiling values into the fixture set so retuning is a versioned event.

## 4. Density

**Expected math.** No standard exists. Closest documented analog: DaVinci Resolve 19's ColorSlice **density** — subtractive (CMY/film-like) saturation that darkens as it saturates, versus additive RGB saturation that brightens ([Mixing Light, *Deconstructing ColorSlice*](https://mixinglight.com/color-grading-tutorials/deconstructing-davinci-resolve-colorslice/); [Frame.io, *Cinematic color saturation*](https://blog.frame.io/2023/04/03/cinematic-color-saturation-resolve-hsv/)). The physical rationale (denser dye absorbs more, so saturation costs luminance) is documented in those sources; any specific transfer function is *inferred* — Resolve does not publish theirs.

**What Lumen does.** `density` (0–100, default 50, `Sources/LumenCore/Recipe/Recipe.swift:212-219`) blends Saturation's additive chroma push against `subtractivePush` (`ColorEngine.swift:1048-1058`): per-channel signed gamma `γ = 1 + satAmount·1.0` on the ratios `c/maxComponent`, norm restored — absorbing channels deepen, the transmitting one holds, neutrals are exact fixed points. Blend `additive.mix(subtractive, density)` gated on `satAmount > 0 && density > 0` (`:1104-1107`). The gate is disclosed: `densityIsLive == saturation > 0` (`Recipe.swift:235`) drives the panel row's enabled state and is pinned by `testDensityIsLiveExactlyWhereItChangesThePicture` (`ColorScienceTests.swift:564`) — the audit's COLOR-28 (silently inert control) is fixed at head.

**Verdict.** **UNPROVEN.** The model is plausible, physically motivated, and matches the *documented behavior* of the field's reference (saturate-by-darkening, neutral-safe); but it is entirely Lumen's own math (*inferred*), and `subtractivePush` itself has **zero tests at any density** — delete the blend line and only the density-liveness test notices the gate, not the curve (audit COLOR-29, still true; `color.density` is absent from the proof registry). Not a defect; an unverified differentiator.

**Gaps (ranked).** 1. Golden `subtractivePush`: at density 100 / Saturation +50, assert chroma rises AND luminance falls on a saturated patch, neutrals bit-exact, monotone in amount. 2. Register `color.density` in the proof registry (aliveness across 1–100 at fixed +Sat). 3. Fixture the additive/subtractive blend at density 50 for cross-platform stability. 4. Document (user-facing) that Density acts only on saturation *pushes*.

## 5. Protect Skin

**Expected math.** Documented convention: human skin of all ethnicities clusters along one vectorscope hue axis — the NTSC **I-line** (~123° on the graticule); complexion varies along luminance/saturation, not hue ([Pixel Valley Studio, *The Skin Tone Line*](https://pixelvalleystudio.com/pmf-articles/the-skin-tone-line); [Wikipedia, Vectorscope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vectorscope)). Skin-protection semantics in commercial tools: Adobe's Vibrance "protects skin tones" (see §2 sources); Capture One's Skin Tone tool converges hue/saturation/lightness toward a picked target ([Capture One support, *Adjusting skin tones*](https://support.captureone.com/hc/en-us/articles/360002596077-Adjusting-skin-tones); [Capture One blog, uniform skin tones](https://www.captureone.com/blog/get-uniform-skintones)). No vendor documents an attenuation formula — all specifics below are *inferred/Lumen's own*.

**What Lumen does.** `protectSkin` (0–100, default 70, `Recipe.swift:213-219`) computes `protection = 1 − protectSkin/100 · skinWeight(c)` from the **stage input** (`ColorEngine.swift:1070-1071`). `skinWeight` (`:721-740`): OKLCh hue distance from `skinLineDegrees = 56.4` (the I-line transported into OKLab a/b, measured from +a; constant golden-locked, `:218`), band ±10° with smoothstep falloff to 15°, chroma gate, and a plausibility window (L rises 0.05→0.20, falls 0.85→0.98; C < 0.16→0.26) so crushed blacks/blown highlights/fire-engine red on the same axis don't count (`:735-740`). Applied to Vibrance at both signs, to Saturation's push only — a pull to −100 still reaches true B&W (`:1090-1092`). The 56.4° constant is corroborated two independent ways in-repo (vectorscope re-derivation ≈56.4°, measured skin swatches 43–63° centroid ≈55°, per `:203-217`) and pinned behaviorally (`ColorScienceTests.swift:387-441`: seven real skin swatches incl. Macbeth light-skin outscore three saturated reds; neutrals rejected). Attenuation path tested: `testProtectSkinAttenuatesTheMovesItSaysItDoes` (`ColorScienceTests.swift:499`) and `testSaturationMinus100ReachesTrueBlackAndWhiteOnSkin` (`:469`) — audit items COLOR-25/26 are fixed at head.

**Verdict.** **CORRECT** in convention (I-line axis, ethnicity-invariant hue, protection-of-pushes semantics) with the axis constant verified against real chromaticities in-repo; the falloff widths and plausibility windows are *inferred* tuning with no external standard. The asymmetric sign policy (Vibrance guarded both ways, Saturation only on push) is a defensible, internally-argued design divergence from a naive "attenuate everything" reading; nothing external contradicts it.

**Gaps (ranked).** 1. `color.protectSkin` is not in the proof registry — add aliveness/authority evidence. 2. No real-photo validation (RAW fixtures with faces) that default-70 leaves a visible-but-plausible push on skin rather than none. 3. ~~Masked local Sat/Vibrance inherit invisible defaults~~ CLOSED (COLOR-27): both local paths now build their colour stage through `ColorAdjust.local(vibrance:saturation:inheriting:)`, inheriting density and protectSkin from the GLOBAL colour panel — the invisible 70 became the photographer's own slider (untouched panels render identically; the defaults are 50/70). A dedicated per-mask override remains a wire-format addition, unranked. 4. The plausibility windows deserve one measured citation (e.g., Macbeth skin L/C ranges) or an explicit "tuned" marker.

## 6. HSL Mixer (hue/sat/lum per band)

**Expected math.** Documented: LR's Color Mixer/HSL has exactly eight bands — red, orange, yellow, green, aqua, blue, purple, magenta — each with Hue/Saturation/Luminance sliders ±100; Hue shifts the band's hue toward neighbors, Luminance lightens/darkens the band ([Adobe LrC Help, Color Mixer](https://helpx.adobe.com/lightroom-classic/desktop/process-and-develop-photos/color-mixer.html) — via excerpt; [Julieanne Kost, Color Mixer in LrC](https://jkost.com/blog/2024/08/using-color-mixer-to-adjust-hue-saturation-and-luminance-in-lightroom-classic.html)). Adobe does not publish band centers, widths, falloff, or what ±100 Hue means in degrees — all *inferred*. DxO/darktable analogs (per-channel uniformity, color zones) are documented features but likewise without math.

**What Lumen does.** Eight bands in LR's wire order (`ColorEngine.swift:96-98`), centers = OKLCh hue of sRGB red (29.23°) + n·45° (`:102-115`), core ±22.5° tiling the circle exactly, raised-cosine feather 15° per side, user-draggable per-side core/feather handles clamped so the partition of unity survives (`:122-143, 464-492`); weights are a normalized C¹ partition, Σw = 1 everywhere (`bandWeights`, `:506-543`) — the structural answer to LR PV-era banding. Per band: Hue ±100 = ±45° (exactly the adjacent band centre, `:146,883`); Sat gain `1 + Σw·s` (linear, 0…2, `:884,896`); Lum: `L += Σw·l · lumShape(L)` with chroma held **literally constant** (`:898-902`), `lumShape = t(1−t)` with the argument clamped at the 0.5 peak so authority *holds* above scene-white instead of dying (`:844-849`, rationale `:149-171` — the audit's COLOR-04 dead-highlights defect is fixed at head). Chroma gate `smoothstep(0.02, 0.06)` scales magnitude, never membership (`:179-180, 554-556`). Uniformity: one global 0–100 slider (`Recipe.swift:496-518`), variance-compression toward the band's target hue (`varianceCompress`, `:589-604`); target = measured chroma-weighted circular mean when supplied, else the user's core-arc midpoint (`:614-620`); the measurement function exists and is tested (`measureBandMeanHues`, `:634-713`; `ColorScienceTests.swift:811-891`) but **`RenderPlan` never passes it** (`RenderPlan.swift:139-141`), and the shipping path is the flat-neighborhood case (localMean = pixel, `:338-365`).

**Verdict.** Mechanism **CORRECT-to-superior** where convention exists (8 LR bands ✓, ±100 ranges ✓, smooth overlap vs LR banding — deliberate improvement; chroma-held Luminance — deliberate, internally documented divergence from LR's visible desaturation, defensible). Two **DIVERGES — defect-class parity gaps**: (a) band *placement* is geometric (29.23° + 45°k), not perceptual — sRGB orange sits ~21° off the "Orange" centre, pure green lands on the Yellow/Green core boundary, foliage falls inside Yellow's core (audit COLOR-01, unchanged at head): dragging "Green" moves foliage at roughly half authority, which breaks LR muscle memory the product bar promises; (b) Uniformity converges on the wrong target on the shipping path (measured means unwired, COLOR-06) and is texture-destroying rather than texture-preserving (flat local mean, COLOR-07 — honestly documented in-code with the wiring plan). **UNPROVEN**: the 24 band sliders now have proof-registry aliveness (`ProofRegistry.swift:436`) and band-geometry/uniformity tests, but no colorimetric golden pins "Blue-band Lum −100 darkens a sky without desaturating it" — the single most-marketed claim.

**Gaps (ranked).** 1. Fix or formally accept band placement: either move centres to perceptual color-name anchors (a pipelineVersion bump — the anchor is golden-locked, `:100-102`) or ship per-band default core offsets that put orange/green where photographers expect. 2. Golden the chroma-held Luminance invariant (ΔC = 0 under band Lum) and hue-stability under band Sat. 3. Wire `measureBandMeanHues` into `RenderPlan` (plan is written at `ColorEngine.swift:56-71,352-365`) — until then Uniformity is a different tool than docs/05 specifies. 4. Uniformity is one global slider captioned per-band (COLOR-05) — align caption or model. 5. `mixer.uniformity` absent from the proof registry.

## 7. Point Color

**Expected math.** Documented LR feature contract (Adobe publishes behavior, not math): sample an exact color; adjust its H/S/L; a master **Range** slider widens/narrows the affected neighborhood with visible feathering; a disclosure exposes independent Hue/Saturation/Luminance range falloffs; Visualize Range grays out unaffected colors ([Julieanne Kost, *Everything you need to know about Point Color*](https://jkost.com/blog/2023/10/everything-you-need-to-know-about-point-color-in-lightroom-classic.html); [Lightroom Killer Tips, *Get to know Point Color*](https://lightroomkillertips.com/get-to-know-point-color-in-lightroom-classic/)). All falloff shapes are *inferred* — Adobe publishes none.

**What Lumen does.** Swatch = working-space RGB sample + master range 0–100 + variance ±100 + HSL shift (`Recipe.swift:583-636`). Compiled once: σ = (0.60, 0.25, 0.30)·range with floor 1e-4 (`ColorEngine.swift:184-189, 920-945`). Per pixel (`applySwatch`, `:963-1010`): normalized ellipsoidal distance in (ΔL, ΔC, ΔH) with **chordal ΔH** `2√(C₁C₂)·sin(Δh/2)` — the CIEDE2000-style form, so hue distance collapses correctly near neutral (`:969-972`); weight = plateau to d=0.5 then smoothstep to 0 at d=1 (`:980`); shifts: hue += w·shiftH (clamped ±60°, `:189,928`), chroma scaled, lightness through the same chroma-preserving `lumShape` kernel as the Mixer (`:1005-1008`). Variance uses the shared kernel at β = 1/1/0.5 for h/C/L (`:987-1003`), flat-neighborhood on the shipping path (same limitation as Mixer Uniformity). Swatches compose in creation order, one pass (`:947-961`). All three axes share the master Range — the per-axis refine is a **documented format gap** (`:936-937`).

**Verdict.** **UNPROVEN with defensible internals.** The distance/falloff design is sound and better-founded than a naive HSL box (chordal ΔH is the standard colorimetric form — documented in CIEDE2000 literature; the rest is *inferred*). Against LR's documented feature set it **DIVERGES by omission**: no per-axis Hue/Sat/Lum range falloffs, no Visualize Range, no 2D hue×sat refinement surface (audit COLOR-12, unchanged). The ±60° hue clamp is consistent panel↔engine (COLOR-13). Behaviorally: proof-registry aliveness now covers hue/sat/lum/range/variance (`ProofRegistry.swift:726-771`), but no golden exercises the falloff *shape*, Variance ≠ 0, or the eyedropper's sampling tap — which is known-inconsistent: the picker samples post-S6 while the engine compares post-S7/S8 stage input, so a swatch picked with non-zero tone sliders selects a slightly different color than clicked (audit COLOR-11, unresolved).

**Gaps (ranked).** 1. Fix the eyedropper tap (sample the colour stage's true input). 2. Per-axis refine ranges — wire-format addition, already flagged in-code. 3. Golden the plateau-then-rolloff weight and the ΔH chordal metric (a neutral-adjacent pixel must be immune to a saturated swatch). 4. Visualize Range parity. 5. ## 8. B&W Mixer

**Expected math.** Documented: LR's B&W Mix is eight luminance-contribution sliders on the same band vocabulary (red…magenta), controlling how each color maps to gray ([Adobe LrC Help](https://helpx.adobe.com/lightroom-classic/help/image-tone-color.html) — via excerpt; [Adobe Community on the B&W panel](https://community.adobe.com/t5/lightroom-classic/black-and-white-part-of-quot-hsl-color-black-and-white-quot-is-missing/m-p/9861474)). The channel-mixer tradition: monochrome = weighted sum of channels, weights summing to preserve overall level ([RawPedia, Channel Mixer](https://rawpedia.rawtherapee.com/Channel_Mixer); [RawPedia, Black-and-White](https://rawpedia.rawtherapee.com/Black-and-White_addon)). Correct grayscale luminance requires the working space's own Y row, not transcribed Rec.709 weights (documented: SMPTE RP 177 derivation; in-repo `ColorSpaces.swift:80-91`).

**What Lumen does.** `look.bw` = 8 bands ±100 + `enabled` (`Sources/LumenCore/Recipe/RecipeLook.swift:462-523`; pipelineVersion 2 keeps a switched-off mix in the recipe — the audit's COLOR-20 cross-photo stash leak is fixed at head, `Recipe.swift:14-26`). `applyBlackAndWhite` (`ColorEngine.swift:1143-1171`): base = Rec.2020-derived luminance; gain = `1 + Σ wᵢ·gate·(bandᵢ/100)` with `bwKappa = 1` so −100 takes a band's gray to zero (`:173`); band weights are the same smooth partition as the Mixer but at **canonical** geometry (deliberate: the mix lives in the portable Look; the Mixer's ring handles are per-photo develop state, `:1150-1160`); chroma gate keeps neutrals from being pushed by a band. Output is an equal-energy triple at the mixed luminance (`:1168-1170`).

**Verdict.** **CORRECT** in convention (8-band LR vocabulary; per-space luminance weights — the classic desaturation bug engineered out; smooth bands vs banding) with one *inferred* choice — `gain` is hue-band-weighted luminance scaling rather than LR's (unpublished) mapping, so exact slider-for-slider LR parity is neither claimed nor possible to verify. The canonical-vs-user-arcs split is a defensible design decision, documented in-code, with no LR analog to diverge from. **Partially UNPROVEN**: one behavioral test exists (`ColorScienceTests.swift:960` — off-state renders color, on-state reaches a true neutral) plus proof aliveness per band (`ProofRegistry.swift:894`), but nothing pins band *sign/selectivity* (Blue −80 darkens a blue patch, leaves a red one) — audit COLOR-22 half-addressed.

**Gaps (ranked).** 1. Band-selectivity golden (per-band sign and locality on known patches). 2. −100 → zero-gray endpoint test. 3. Feature parity: no Auto mix, no TAT (COLOR-21). 4. Document that a B&W frame still runs the color Mixer upstream (it does, by design — state it in user docs).

## 9. Color-Grade Wheels (shadow/mid/high + global, luminance, blending, advanced grid)

**Expected math.**
- *ASC CDL* — documented: `out = (in·slope + offset)^power` per channel, applied in S-O-P order ([Wikipedia, ASC CDL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASC_CDL); [Pomfort, *An in-depth look at ASC-CDL based color controls*](https://pomfort.com/article/an-in-depth-look-at-asc-cdl-based-color-controls/)).
- *darktable color balance rgb* — documented: 4-way (global/shadows/mid/highlights); lift ≈ CDL-style multiply masked to shadows, gain = CDL slope masked to highlights, power = CDL power normalized at a white fulcrum, offset unmasked; perceptual **saturation** and **brilliance** run in a UCS (JzAzBz, later darktable UCS), saturation at constant brightness ray, brilliance orthogonal, chroma at constant lightness ([darktable 4.8 manual, color balance rgb](https://docs.darktable.org/usermanual/4.8/en/module-reference/processing-modules/color-balance-rgb/) — via excerpt; [darktable manual, color dimensions](https://docs.darktable.org/usermanual/4.0/en/special-topics/color-management/color-dimensions/) — via excerpt; corroborating issue discussion [darktable#12163](https://github.com/darktable-org/darktable/issues/12163)).
- *Adobe Color Grading* — documented behavior: three wheels + Global; hue by angle, saturation by radius; a Luminance slider per wheel; **Blending** softens/overlaps the zone boundaries; **Balance** shifts which tones count as shadows vs highlights ([Adobe, Color grading in LrC](https://pages.adobe.com/creativecloud/en/photography-plan/lightroom/color-grading-classic); [CaptureLandscapes, Color Grading tool](https://www.capturelandscapes.com/color-grading-tool-in-lightroom/)). Adobe publishes no zone-weight math — specifics *inferred*.

**What Lumen does.** Explicitly **neither** CDL nor lift/gamma/gain (header, `Sources/LumenCore/Engine/GradeEngine.swift:6-21`): a wheel is a constant-luminance OKLab (a,b) translation, magnitude ≤ 0.15 at full deflection (`WheelTint`, `:201-223`; `maxABOffset`, `:232`); its Luminance ring is a perceptual H-K brightness gain of ±0.5 stops through `LumenUCS.scaleBrightness` (`:234, 459-464`; `Perceptual.swift:222-232`), chroma-ratio-preserving. Zones: two visible pivots (defaults 0.33/0.67 on the normalized log₂(Y/0.18) axis anchored −9…+5 EV) with raised-cosine crossfades; Blending 0–100 maps to half-width (1.5 EV at 50) eased onto the half-separation ceiling via `softKnee` so the top fifth of the slider isn't dead (`ZoneWindows`, `:56-195`; knee rationale `:129-148`); Balance ±100 translates both pivots ±2 EV (`:59-60, 119-122`); weights sum to exactly 1; zone position measured on the **stage input** (invariant 4, `:431-436`). A closed-form monotonicity solve (`solveLumScale`, `:315-362`) caps the zone-weighted luminance term so opposing wheels at hard Blending cannot invert tone response, eased so the ring never goes dead; global luminance rides outside the cap; total stops clamped ±2 (`:444-446`). Advanced grid (`ColorBalanceGrid`, `:645-784`): master hue rotation (constant L, C) and vibrance, then chroma (C·g at constant L,h), saturation (s = C/L scaled at constant H-K brightness — one quadratic via the affine H-K slope, `:727-742`), brilliance (B·g at constant ratio, `:746-761`), spread over the same zone windows; on the shipping path and baked (`:263`, tests `ColorScienceTests.swift:600-697`). Wire: `RecipeLook.swift:175-302` (hue-not-scaled by mask Amount for wheels, scaled for grid hueShift — both argued in-code).

**Verdict.** **DIVERGES — deliberate, documented, defensible.** Against both reference conventions (CDL S-O-P; darktable's masked lift/gain + power) Lumen substitutes constant-L tint translation + perceptual gain; the in-code claim that shadow/mid/high offsets "behave like lift/gamma/gain" is explicitly a tooltip-level equivalence, not math — correctly attributed as such (`:16-21`). The grid's chroma/saturation/brilliance taxonomy **matches darktable's documented three-way distinction** (the only other implementation in the field) while substituting OKLab+Nayatani for JzAzBz/dt-UCS — same intent, different UCS, *inferred* equivalence only. Blending/Balance semantics match Adobe's documented descriptions qualitatively; the EV denominations (±2 EV balance, 1.5 EV half-width) are Lumen's own. Verified at head: blending dead-zone fix (`RobustnessTests.swift:1088`), circular hue continuity (`:1237`), zone/lumScale fixtures (`EngineMathFixtureTests.swift:267`), grid reach/bake/zone-selectivity/three-different-moves tests, wheel aliveness in the proof registry. The masked-grade window contradiction is CLOSED (COLOR-16): both paths now pass the mask's wheels through `GradingWheels.adoptingWindows(from:)`, taking pivots, blending and balance from the GLOBAL wheels — the docs/08 §8.4 contract as written. Structural conviction in MaskingTests: moving only the global pivots must change what a masked grade does near a zone boundary.

**Gaps (ranked).** 1. ~~Resolve COLOR-16~~ RESOLVED — the global windows now reach the masked grade (`adoptingWindows(from:)`, both paths). 2. Grid axes: `colorBalance.*` fields are absent from the proof registry. 3. A published equivalence note (what a Lumen wheel setting corresponds to in CDL terms at mid-gray) if "colorists can read it" is a goal. 4. Luminance ring's H-K claim (equal perceived-brightness change across hues) has no test. 5. The histogram strip draws zone weights, not the image's histogram (spec wants histogram; minor).

## 10. Printer Lights

**Expected math.** Documented: a printer point is 1/12 stop = 0.025 log₁₀ exposure; 12 points = one full stop; per-channel R/G/B trims plus master, applied as relative exposure to the print — the convention Resolve's offset/printer-lights hotkeys reproduce ([Wikipedia, Printer point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Printer_point); [Lowepost, *Master study in DaVinci Resolve printer lights*](https://lowepost.com/insider/colorgrading/master-study-in-davinci-resolve-printer-lights-r33/); [PremiumBeat, printer light hotkeys](https://www.premiumbeat.com/blog/printer-light-hotkeys-in-davinci-resolve/)). Caveat, documented in the same sources: historical lab practice varied (some systems ~8 points/stop); 0.025 logE/12-per-stop is the canonical Kodak/Resolve convention.

**What Lumen does.** Integer points on the wire, master ±48, trims ±24 (`RecipeLook.swift:306-332`); gains `2^((master + channel)/12)` exactly (`GradeEngine.swift:237-241, 388-402`); applied at S6 as scene-linear multiplies fused with WB and exposure (`WhiteBalanceEngine.swift:191-208`, `RenderPlan.swift:124-129`) — i.e. *before* tone, color and the look, which is what "printing lights" means (exposure into the stock, not a post-grade trim). Deliberately not folded into `GradeEngine.apply` to preserve stage order (`GradeEngine.swift:24-27, 385-388`).

**Verdict.** **CORRECT** — exact against the documented convention (2^(points/12), bit-comparable; 12 points = 2.0×), correct placement, integer-steppable, and the best-tested control in the section: twelfth-stop exactness (`EngineIntegrationTests.swift:1326`), S6 fusion (`EngineTests.swift:563`), stage-removal tripwire (`RobustnessTests.swift:1475`), proof-registry aliveness (`ProofRegistry.swift:611-620`). No stills competitor ships this (audit COLOR-18: "best-in-field", verified).

**Gaps (ranked).** 1. On-image ghost readout / keyboard Shift-step variants (UX only). 2. A one-line user-doc note on the 12-vs-8 points-per-stop lab folklore so support threads don't call the math wrong.

## 11. Primaries (hue/purity per channel + Shadows Tint)

**Expected math.** Documented: darktable's *rgb primaries* module — per-primary **hue rotation** and **purity** adjustment "while leaving uncolored (gray) pixels unchanged", equivalent to a re-derived channel-mixer matrix; opponency preserved; usable pre- or post-tone-map ([darktable 4.6 manual, rgb primaries](https://docs.darktable.org/usermanual/4.6/en/module-reference/processing-modules/rgb-primaries/) — via excerpt; [darktable PR #15113](https://github.com/darktable-org/darktable/pull/15113)). Adobe's Calibration panel is the LR analog — Hue/Saturation per R/G/B primary redefining what the primaries mean globally, plus a Shadows Tint slider ([PetaPixel, *Demystifying the Camera Calibration panel*](https://petapixel.com/2017/08/22/demystifying-camera-calibration-panel-lightroom/); [How-To Geek, *What is Calibration in ACR/LR*](https://www.howtogeek.com/748899/what-is-calibration-in-adobe-camera-raw-and-lightroom/)). Neither vendor publishes the matrix math; degrees-per-slider-unit and purity scaling are *inferred* everywhere.

**What Lumen does.** Wire: `rHue/rPurity/gHue/gPurity/bHue/bPurity/tintHue/tintPurity`, all ±100, 0 = identity (`RecipeLook.swift:407-447`). Matrix (`ColorEngine.swift:778-811`): each primary's xy chromaticity offset from the white point is rotated up to ±20° and radially scaled ±50% at full deflection (`:255-256, 790-797`), guarded against leaving the chromaticity plane by directional shrink (`safeChromaticity`, `:817-835`); the RGB→XYZ matrix is rebuilt by the RP 177 construction, which renormalizes rows so `M·(1,1,1)` is the white point — **grays preserved by construction at any setting** (`:774-777`; the property is mathematically forced by the construction, matching darktable's documented "gray pixels unchanged" contract). Applied as S9's first move (`applyPrimaries`, `:744-756`). Shadows Tint: an OKLab (a,b) offset of amplitude ≤0.05 along the green↔magenta opponent axis (328.36°) and its perpendicular (`tintHue`/`tintPurity`), windowed to shadows by a pinned raised-cosine window at −3 EV ± 1.5 EV — deliberately *not* the user's grading pivots (`:257-263, 758-771`).

**Verdict.** **CORRECT** in convention (matches darktable's documented contract — per-primary hue/purity, neutral-axis invariance, matrix formulation — and LR Calibration's control vocabulary including Shadows Tint), with all scaling constants (±20°, ±50%, 0.05 amplitude, −3 EV window, 328.36° axis) **inferred/Lumen's own**. The in-code claim that this "beats LrC Calibration, where extreme primary moves drag neutrals off-axis" is *asserted by nothing* — the gray-preservation half is provable from the construction but untested, and the LrC-drift half is an unverified claim about a competitor (audit COLOR-19; keep it out of user-facing copy until measured). **UNPROVEN** overall: at head, `primaries.*` sliders have proof-registry aliveness (`ProofRegistry.swift:680`), but no test asserts neutral invariance, rotation direction, purity monotonicity, or the shadow window's pinning.

**Gaps (ranked).** 1. Gray-axis golden: for a grid of settings incl. extremes, `apply(gray) == gray` to 1e-12 — cheap, and it converts the headline claim from asserted to proven. 2. Shadows Tint window test (pixels at +0 EV unmoved; −4 EV moved; window immune to grading-pivot changes). 3. Rotation/purity direction goldens against named expectations (bHue +100 twists blue toward cyan ⇒ opponent yellow toward orange — the documented darktable opponency behavior). 4. Measure the LrC-drift comparison or delete the claim. 5. `safeChromaticity`'s bisection fallback (Rec.2020 blue at purity +100) has no test at the boundary it exists for.
